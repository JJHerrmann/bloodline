Scene 1

The Cavern Showdown

December 30, 2025 Act 3 Chapter 44 Scene 1 draft 954 words

Please leave comments on the scene using the form at the end of this section.

"Judah" Sands, CPO, MA, USN (Ret.), December 30th, 2025

My back hit the stone altar with a wet thud. I felt the stab in my mid-back. Breath, stab, exhale, stab. He'd broken at least two ribs in that hit. My desperate panting became even more shallow as my head swam from the vicious backhand.

Arthmon roared, and I felt it, down in my bones, my teeth rattling in my skull, reverberating as if a hell-borne tyrannosaurus was attempting to make a point about its territory. But I was between it and the boys. I may not save them, but I for sure wasn't going to make it easy on the corrupted spirit to try to take either of them.

I struggled to my feet, the world spinning dangerously as my vision danced, almost putting me back on my ass. I put a hand on the altar to steady myself. My mouth and sinuses were tinged with the copper smell and taste of blood. He’d gotten me good with that one.

I locked eyes with the predatory glare from crimson eyes under that deer-skull helm. He was cast in a dancing and uncanny contrast, the low fires of candles around the cavern giving him an almost impossible inky blackness anywhere that wasn’t immediately hit by the light. Hawk-talon hands arched in a readied attack posture, blackened talons seeming to scream for blood under them. Beneath the fetid skin and patchy fur, muscles twitched and flexed, like it was trying to decide which direction it wanted to go—fight or flight. I suspected that the little bit of resistance in its motions, the jerking and disconcerting movements, were possibly my grandfather trying to rein control of the beast.

Even the glare behind the crimson eyes danced between recognition and threat assessment.

But I couldn't worry about that right now. I readied myself for it to attack when a blast rang in the cavern, causing my ears to ring and warping my equilibrium even more. The ring was enough to make my eyelids flex in response, the tension behind my eyes almost equal to high pitched wine. Arthmon snarled and roared again, and I saw the puckers of blood rising to the surface of its matted frame. A hit.

"Leave. My. Fucking. Kids. ALONE," a familiar voice demanded—Levi, with a shotgun, that he racked another slug into defiantly.

My brother stood off to my right, his flame-red hair nearly a match for the enraged look in his eyes as he stared the creature down, unblinking, almost predatory in his own right. He’d come with a small armory, the wide flap pockets of an olive canvas field jacket bulky and irregular, probably stuffed for bear with shells. Same for the cargo pockets of the black pants he wore over hiking boots.

Arthmon snarled, exposing long yellowed and sickly canines under ounces of viscous drool. A whisper of fetid flesh and viscera escaped it’s throat and lingered around us, promising a quick death for us, and maybe a meal for it.

I felt a touch against my shoulder and nearly wheeled and fell from surprise. Ryan stood next to me, his finger to his lips, a rifle in his hand warily directed toward the beast. "Wondered where you got to," he said in a breathless whisper.

"Well—" I croaked, the words straining against the stabbing pain in my side. "—you know me. Keeping busy."

The cool metal of a pistol pressed into my hand. "Thought you might want a corsage for the dance and all," Ryan said, his eyes not leaving Arthmon.

"I appreciate it. But there's only room for two on this card. Get the boys and go."

"My guy, I'm not—"

Pop-pop-pop. Suddenly the dusty cavern floor in front of us jumped alive, small puffs of dust and rock leaping into the air as a rifle report rang through the enclosed space. Cordite and vaporized particulates of lead added to the metallic tang in the air and set my reflexes ablaze. Almost without thinking I pulled myself over the altar, grabbing and dragging Tyler with me and collapsing painfully on the other side, Ryan sliding next to me holding Dylan in his arms.

"Looky here. Two Sands, a big ol’ beasty, and the stray they fancy." I knew that voice. Walton. I heard Ryan growl in recognition as well as I risked a peek around the altar and saw the former deputy dressed in sheriff’s khaki, with a pair of men I didn’t recognize on a higher path that led toward the top of the cavern.

Arthmon roared again and leapt into action, shooting past Levi and toward the new intruders. The beast moved in an odd syncopated rhythm, its shorter hind legs taping nearly twice for each land of it’s massive clawed hands as it sprung up the wall to the pathway above, getting between the intruders and the exit they’d come from.

Dozens of shots rang out in the enclosed space, causing my head to spin. Suddenly the dust kicked and Levi was with us, sliding behind the pillar to escape the barrage of gunfire. "What the fuck is all that!?"

I glanced at him. "Basically? Gestapo for that Ascendency church that ran the clinic you worked for that basically blame us for that—" I gestured back as if to point at Arthmon "—existing."

Levi groaned, loading more shells into his shotgun's rail. "Speaking of, what the fuck is that and why does it have you, Dad, and the boys in this dank cave?"

I crouched lower as bullets hit the top of the altar above us. I locked eyes with my brother and said the simplest version of what I'd learned about Arthmon.

"Our birthright."

Scene 2

The Fall of the Father

December 30, 2025 Act 3 Chapter 44 Scene 2 draft 901 words

Please leave comments on the scene using the form at the end of this section.

Judah Sands Isaac Sands Levi Sands Ryan Thompson George Walton Arthmon (Garnet - Protection) Abraham Sands Tyler Sands Dylan Morgan

I’ve heard less chaotic war zones. But then again, most of the action I’ve seen was either open air or in a building that had furniture and interior walls that dampened sound. A mostly empty stone cavern is a nightmare for awareness—every shot amplified, repeated, echoed, and redirected. Every scream or shout distorted. From our hiding spot behind the altar, it was almost impossible to tell who was winning in the gun battle versus the monster that was my grandfather, outside of the fact that it was still happening, which gave the betting odds to Arthmon.

“Our… birthright?” Levi whisper-shouted over the noise, his attention mostly focused on checking his boys for injuries himself now that he was in cover.

“Best I can word it,” I said, before we hunkered again, another spray of pebbles cascading over the edge of the stone behind us. “Short version? Druids, magic, spirits that get passed down bloodlines—” and I jerked a thumb back toward the chaos “—and Pa.”

Levi just stared at me. “That’s. Insane.”

I didn’t disagree. I pushed myself sideways to peek around the altar. Arthmon had pushed Walton and company farther down the path they were on, closer to the cavern’s main entrance. I didn’t think it was quite a selfless act of protection for us, angling them away from us so the crossfire wouldn’t chew through the altar, but more that Arthmon didn’t want his prizes killed before he could transfer himself from my dying grandfather and continue the bloodline.

I looked at the other side of the room and froze.

“Oh God.”

Isaac Sands—the cryptozoologist, heir of Arthmon, and our father—sat against the wall of the cavern, three dark red circles in his shirt, the glossy wet stains spreading, glare twinkling from the firelight around him in the illuminated chamber.

The chaotic screams of the fight in the cavern stilled as my mind went blank. I was moving before I’d registered that I’d left the safety of our hiding spot, barely registering the tug of my shirt as Ryan and Levi both grabbed for me. I sprinted, the aches and searing pain of my ribs gone as I slid across the stone floor down to my father.

“No no no no no—Dad.”

Another sound of scraping and Levi was next to me, grabbing my father’s arm. “JUDAH, WE GOTTA MOVE!” he shouted to me, my mind barely processing it above the dissociative ring of terror and horror at my father’s condition.

Instinct and training took over then. We couldn’t stay out here; that was asking to get shot too. I looped my father’s other arm over my shoulder and, with Levi, we ran him to the altar, laying him down behind it.

Small favors were that whatever Arthmon had done to Tyler and Dylan, they were still unconscious and didn’t have to see their grandfather like this. Dad’s eyes were glassy, and he stared at the ceiling. The only sign he was still there was the ragged rise and fall of his bullet-ravaged chest, and the pump of blood from the three wide holes in his chest.

The only thing I could hear was the caustic boil of my blood in my ears. There were no tears; I couldn’t afford them, and I was damned for it. Levi was busy at work, desperately ripping off his over shirt and attempting to plug and apply pressure to the wounds as skillfully as someone that had binged a medical procedural far too much. It was too much. Too bad. With being out here with Arthmon for weeks? There was a fatalism to thinking about it, and right now my brain was refusing to do it, cheering my brother on.

I stood—far more gracefully than I had been managing—spun, and fired, the gun kicking in my hand, muzzle flash lighting the dust, dropping one of the khaki-clad assailants as Arthmon pressured them with powerful swipes and roars. I gave a roar of my own before I was suddenly jerked back down by my collar. Ryan was inches away, looking me eye to eye, and taking the gun from me. He glanced down at Isaac to draw my attention.

Levi was kneeling over our father, grasping one of his hands. I kneeled down as my father pawed the ground around me, lacing my fingers in his. One squeeze. Two squeezes. Tears rolled down my father’s face as he locked on to first me, and then Levi. The third squeeze faltered, the fingers spasming halfway through the motion.

His chest collapsed under its own weight, no longer strong enough to rise again.

The exhale was ragged and final. A small smile crossed my father’s lips as he moved his eyes finally to the cavern ceiling, and then he stopped moving, the hand going limp in mine. The warm, blood-slicked finger twitched once against my palm.

The cavern forgot to echo—or I forgot to hear it. The temperature around me dropped as quickly as my heart in that moment, watching the dust and movement of my father settle into the release of oblivion.

From behind us, a raw howl of anguish rose from the monster’s throat. Inside the monster, inside that corrupted form, I could almost hear Abraham—grieving through the creature that had devoured him.

Scene 3

Tyr’s Triumph

December 31, 2025 Act 3 Chapter 44 Scene 3 draft 1232 words

Please leave comments on the scene using the form at the end of this section.

There’s a certain clarity that comes from emotions if you know how to use them. People always seem to think that there’s an operator state where you don’t feel the emotion of the moment, and instead have this clear headed drive to accomplish a goal. They are wrong. That clarity comes from the overwhelming amount of emotion. Anger, grief, regret.

They make excellent fuel.

I looked at Levi over our father. “We have to get them—” I jerked my thumb to Ryan and the still unconscious boys “—out of here.” Levi looked up at me and then to his sons. He swiped his face with a sleeve and nodded. “What’s the play?”

“Bait.” I didn’t wait to see if Levi understood or not, he’s a smart lad, he’ll get the idea. I rose up from behind the altar and started moving purposefully to my left, away from the path that led up, hopefully to the outside, firing as I did, the muzzle flashing and kicking in my palm as I lined Arthmon’s back up, and landed several admittedly ineffective shots.

The beast growled and turned to face me as I continued to follow the wall, moving towards the cavern entrance away from the altar. Come on pup, follow me… I thought, willing Arthmon to keep it’s gaze on me. To it’s credit it matched my turn, keeping itself facing me as I moved around the room. It dragged Walton with it, the man screaming as he was tugged by a bloodied ruined leg. As I got Arthmon’s back to the altar, I saw a flash of motion, a bouncing wave of ginger skirting down the slope from the upper path to behind the altar. Temperance. She must have either come with Ryan or followed him. I didn’t really have time to keep that thought as suddenly Levi appeared from above Arthmon, jumping on it’s back and locking the barrel of the shotgun under it’s snout like a bridle as the creature bolted into a rodeo of violence and screams.

I couldn’t shoot towards Levi to keep Arthmon’s attention, and anything too fancy risked hitting Temperance or the boys if they were trying to make an exit.

I chose the dumbest next best thing. I strode across the cavern to the beast just in time to have to throw myself sideways to avoid Walton, who had found himself being Arthmon’s weapon of choice in trying to remove Levi from it’s back.

On the ground I lashed a boot out at a padded foot near me and was surprised to find that it gave. sliding inward and knocking Arthmon off balance. Then my world went white and black and I could taste more blood in my mouth and nose. I’d been on the receiving end of Walton, who had curled as well as he could into a ball to protect his vital parts. Good for him. He was wailing louder than Arthmon’s roar, and the cold stone floor felt nice against my ravaged head. I felt another thud as a body collapsed beside me. Levi.

We looked at each other and then at Arthmon, who was crossing the gap between us with a scary predatory efficiency. The creature looked triumphant, as the last obstacles in it’s transference were ready to be removed from the equation.

Suddenly a thundercrack sounded across all of the walls of the cavern at once as Ryan unloaded the discarded shotgun into the small of Arthmon’s back, the slugs apparently having more impact the longer this dragged out.

“HEY OVER HERE YOU RANCID MUTT.” He shouted tensely, throwing the emptied shotgun to the side and switching to the rifle he’d brought. Drawing Arthmon’s attention, who even went so far as to discard Walton in turning it’s Ire towards Ryan.

“Le– Levi” I said, my word stopping and starting as I tried to get used to using my Jaw in its new position. “We aren’t going to win this fighting with it. We need to be smarter.” I reached in my shirt and pulled the Garnet plinth off my head. “This is the key to it’s transferring. I can’t use it. Not the right blood type or something it said.”

Levi looked at the gem. Then to me. “But I am?”

“I honestly have no idea, but I’m out of ideas and almost out of bones to break up in this.”

Levi sighed. “What do I do?”

“Blood. It’s and yours. Luckily we are all banged up enough for that to be in abundance.” The gem throbbed in my hand. I couldn’t tell if it was trying to get me to release it and trying to cause an ache, or if that was just… well all the rest of it. As much as I thought of myself as a protector, apparently that’s not what was meant for me.

Levi finally groaned and grabbed the stone, clambering to his feet as Ryan’s rifle fell silent empty. “HEY SCRAPPY! LOOKING FOR THIS?” Arthmon whirled a single crimson eye back towards us, and locked on to the garnet. The key. It decided that not having that in hand was more of an issue than Ryan, turning and bounding towards Levi.

Levi moved forward, taking a step, twisting his torso, and surprising Arthmon. And me. And himself. he punched the monster in the mouth. Like those videos of a diver and a great white shark. Arthmon stopped for a moment, perplexed at the audacity.

That moment did not last. The various animal parts twitched, first in surprise and confusion, and then what for all the world could be called indignation. Levi didn’t face away from it. Defiantly glaring at the beast, despite the futility he’d just displayed. It was then I noticed Temperance, dragging both boys up the path away from the scene. If anything, Levi was doing his level best to keep Arthmon’s attention as she made sure what he cared about got out of the beast’s grasp.

Then the most natural response, Arthmon lunged forward and bit Levi’s arm almost to the elbow. Levi screamed and beat at the skull with his other arm, a menacing chuckle rasping from the ruined creature’s throat. Suddenly it yelped. Like a dog stung by a bee.

The room had a beat of silence as the three of us looked at the creature in confusion.

Then it started. The pressure in the room changed, it felt heavy. I felt my body pressed to the stone floor, and I could see Ryan on his knees on the other side of the scene. The candles all flickered like a gale had blown through the cavern, and the sigils in the wall began to glow, flooding the blue pigments that my father had thought to be woad in his notes with a garnet colored light.

The wind switched and changed, and it felt like being caught in a sandstorm. Arthmon’s fur twisted and blew in each direction, the snakeskin shedding and sloughing off of its arms. Levi was still screaming, and now so was Arthmon as the various animal parts of him began to desiccate, drying up and crumbling away. Until nothing but Abraham remained, naked, and stone dead. Levi stood there looking at the stump of his arm numbly. The separated appendage lay on the floor, grasping the blood coated garnet which glowed brightly as whatever just happened completed.
