The Cavern Showdown

December 30, 2025 Act 3 Chapter 44 Scene 1 draft 954 words

"Judah" Sands, CPO, MA, USN (Ret.), December 30th, 2025

My back hit the stone altar with a wet thud. I felt the stab in my mid-back. Breath, stab, exhale, stab. He'd broken at least two ribs in that hit. My desperate panting became even more shallow as my head swam from the vicious backhand.

Arthmon roared, and I felt it, down in my bones, my teeth rattling in my skull, reverberating as if a hell-borne tyrannosaurus was attempting to make a point about its territory. But I was between it and the boys. I may not save them, but I for sure wasn't going to make it easy on the corrupted spirit to try to take either of them.

I struggled to my feet, the world spinning dangerously as my vision danced, almost putting me back on my ass. I put a hand on the altar to steady myself. My mouth and sinuses were tinged with the copper smell and taste of blood. He’d gotten me good with that one.

I locked eyes with the predatory glare from crimson eyes under that deer-skull helm. He was cast in a dancing and uncanny contrast, the low fires of candles around the cavern giving him an almost impossible inky blackness anywhere that wasn’t immediately hit by the light. Hawk-talon hands arched in a readied attack posture, blackened talons seeming to scream for blood under them. Beneath the fetid skin and patchy fur, muscles twitched and flexed, like it was trying to decide which direction it wanted to go—fight or flight. I suspected that the little bit of resistance in its motions, the jerking and disconcerting movements, were possibly my grandfather trying to rein control of the beast.

Even the glare behind the crimson eyes danced between recognition and threat assessment.

But I couldn't worry about that right now. I readied myself for it to attack when a blast rang in the cavern, causing my ears to ring and warping my equilibrium even more. The ring was enough to make my eyelids flex in response, the tension behind my eyes almost equal to high pitched wine. Arthmon snarled and roared again, and I saw the puckers of blood rising to the surface of its matted frame. A hit.

"Leave. My. Fucking. Kids. ALONE," a familiar voice demanded—Levi, with a shotgun, that he racked another slug into defiantly.

My brother stood off to my right, his flame-red hair nearly a match for the enraged look in his eyes as he stared the creature down, unblinking, almost predatory in his own right. He’d come with a small armory, the wide flap pockets of an olive canvas field jacket bulky and irregular, probably stuffed for bear with shells. Same for the cargo pockets of the black pants he wore over hiking boots.

Arthmon snarled, exposing long yellowed and sickly canines under ounces of viscous drool. A whisper of fetid flesh and viscera escaped it’s throat and lingered around us, promising a quick death for us, and maybe a meal for it.

I felt a touch against my shoulder and nearly wheeled and fell from surprise. Ryan stood next to me, his finger to his lips, a rifle in his hand warily directed toward the beast. "Wondered where you got to," he said in a breathless whisper.

"Well—" I croaked, the words straining against the stabbing pain in my side. "—you know me. Keeping busy."

The cool metal of a pistol pressed into my hand. "Thought you might want a corsage for the dance and all," Ryan said, his eyes not leaving Arthmon.

"I appreciate it. But there's only room for two on this card. Get the boys and go."

"My guy, I'm not—"

*Pop-pop-pop.* Suddenly the dusty cavern floor in front of us jumped alive, small puffs of dust and rock leaping into the air as a rifle report rang through the enclosed space. Cordite and vaporized particulates of lead added to the metallic tang in the air and set my reflexes ablaze. Almost without thinking I pulled myself over the altar, grabbing and dragging Tyler with me and collapsing painfully on the other side, Ryan sliding next to me holding Dylan in his arms.

"Looky here. Two Sands, a big ol’ beasty, and the stray they fancy." I knew that voice. Walton. I heard Ryan growl in recognition as well as I risked a peek around the altar and saw the former deputy dressed in sheriff’s khaki, with a pair of men I didn’t recognize on a higher path that led toward the top of the cavern.

Arthmon roared again and leapt into action, shooting past Levi and toward the new intruders. The beast moved in an odd syncopated rhythm, its shorter hind legs taping nearly twice for each land of it’s massive clawed hands as it sprung up the wall to the pathway above, getting between the intruders and the exit they’d come from.

Dozens of shots rang out in the enclosed space, causing my head to spin. Suddenly the dust kicked and Levi was with us, sliding behind the pillar to escape the barrage of gunfire. "What the fuck is all that!?"

I glanced at him. "Basically? Gestapo for that Ascendency church that ran the clinic you worked for that basically blame us for that—" I gestured back as if to point at Arthmon "—existing."

Levi groaned, loading more shells into his shotgun's rail. "Speaking of, what the fuck is that and why does it have you, Dad, and the boys in this dank cave?"

I crouched lower as bullets hit the top of the altar above us. I locked eyes with my brother and said the simplest version of what I'd learned about Arthmon.

"Our birthright."
