Guns, Agents, and Assholes

November 19, 2025 Act 1 Chapter 5 Scene 2 draft 1115 words

Please leave comments on the scene using the form at the end of the page.

“Who the fuck are you?” said the gruff voice from behind the gun. My eyes worked to adjust to the newcomer’s flashlight in my face. I saw a scruffy-looking man in a deep blue windbreaker with a trio of white reflective letters reading “SBI” on the left chest. He was wearing jeans over some less than practical boots, his dark hair thinning and a very tired look accentuating the 10 p.m.’s worth of five-o’clock shadow across his jaw. He wasn’t tall, but not short either, maybe around 5’8”. The intimidation factor of being at the business end of his pistol was making him seem taller. Probably also the slope.

“Hi. I’m Judah Sands, Consultant for the Deep Springs Police Department?” I said hopefully sounding truthful despite still being somewhat in a state of disbelief about that particular situation. “Also Chief Petty Officer Master at Arms at Naval Station Norfolk, retired?” Who knows, that might also help.

“Wait, When the fuck did you—?” I heard from the ridge, and looked up to see Ryan with his own pistol drawn and pointed down at us. He then froze and relaxed, putting his own hands up and showing his finger off the trigger to someone behind him as another voice, this one female said “Where the fuck do you—”

“WHY THE FUCK IS EVERYONE POINTING GUNS AT EACH OTHER?” Another very tired, very winded voice shouted, and even in the near twilight, I could see the corpulent, sweat-sheened outline of Samuel Evans huffing and puffing his way to the edge of the trail. “HEY. If I’d known you’d just start pointing guns at anybody you’d see I’d not have helped you get into the county around Coop.”

I stopped myself from laughing at the older man’s obvious frustration, if nothing else than to keep from startling the man in front of me threatening to pick my nose with a bullet.

“Evenin’ Chief Sam.” Ryan said, not moving. “Who do we have the privilege of working with this auspicious evening?” His tone was deadpan, I guess he too was also just trying to not accidentally escalate our new friends. I didn’t know Sam Evans was the chief of anything. Must be over in Jewel Hill where he had been running a hardware store for years before I’d left town.

The man in front of me sniffed and lowered his gun finally. “Sorry. It’s been a day. Agent Henry Lee.”

“Oh like Lighthor-”

“Don’t. I hate that, I’m holding a gun, and just bushwhacked my way here unexpectedly.” He holstered his gun and I saw Ryan put his hands down in my peripheral, suggesting the other, I assumed, SBI agent had also put hers down.

“I get it. My dad hates getting Ram plushies for the same reason.” I said. “I’m going for an evidence bag in my pocket, just so we don’t get twitchy again,” The agent nodded to me. Reaching into my pocket, I fished one out, dropped my father’s wallet into it, sealed it and added it to the small collection in my knee pocket. “So you guys are the SBI agents I heard about getting waylaid huh?”

Agent Lee and I picked our way back up the slope to the trail where I got introduced to his partner, Agent Kassie Duilion, a younger agent, with shoulder length blonde hair tied back tightly into a bun. She also wore an SBI windbreaker over jeans and boots, but was a much shorter 5’2” to Agent Lee. She was quiet, issuing a nod of acknowledgement when I introduced myself, but not saying much past her own name. I left her to that, I’m sure I’d not be in much of a mood for talking if I’d struggled to get here around a county sheriff getting big for his britches either.

I turned and faced Samuel Evans, who was still wiping the sweat from his brow. “Mister—,” I paused, reflexively forgetting he wasn’t just Mister Evans the hardware store owner anymore. “—Or I guess I should say, Chief Evans,” I said, reaching a hand out to the Jewel Hill Police Chief.

“Evenin’, Judah. Sorry ‘bout the circumstance, but it’s good to see you anyhow boy.” he said, nodding graciously and accepting my handshake. “You boys find anything up here while we were making our way around Cooper and the deputies?”

So that’s what my grandpa meant by that pointed “if something hadn’t waylaid the SBI” comment.

I spent a minute explaining what I’d found down the hill. The broken sapling, the dried blood, the wallet and journal. “—The only things I do know from looking is that he wasn’t dragged off like he’d died there, it doesn’t look like he got away from there under his own power unless he was being very stealthy, which given the dried blood I found, I’d find hard to believe he was capable of.”

I touched my knee pocket and retrieved the evidence bag for the wallet, then the journal in it’s bag.

“Oh?” Looking at the bottom corner of the plastic bag that held the journal, I could see a slit in the bottom about an inch long, like a manufacturing error missed the seal on the corner. I fished out my phone and snapped a photo of the compromised bag, making sure to get the split corner and the sealed mouth of the bag in my records. The journal itself would have to do for scale. I put my phone back in one pocket and grabbed a fresh bag from the other. I checked the bottom seal and made sure this one didn’t have the same defect, and dropped the journal, compromised evidence bag and all, into the fresh collection bag and sealed it. “Well, it’s not a paper evidence bag, but hopefully we can still get something off that.”

Chief Evans pulled a cell phone out of his pocket, and dialed a number. “David? Yes, I did that and they are up here too. Judah might have gotten something if you can get away and come back down.” After a few more affirmatives and nods, Evans put away the phone. “Alright. So David is on his way back down. I’m going to make myself scarce before Cooper follows him and I have to deal with him being in a bad mood with me all week. So I’ll see you all later, and if you need me I’m just down the road.” And with that he started trudging his way back down the trail towards the road, his round body striding with the indomitable purpose of a man who wanted no smoke from the county sheriff, which I couldn’t blame him for.
