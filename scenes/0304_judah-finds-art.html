Judah Finds Art

November 19, 2025 Act 1 Chapter 3 Scene 4 draft 569 words

The center of town was bustling as I turned on my walk onto the main road. I was headed to the coffee shop I'd noticed with Ryan on the way into the town, both for the coffee and to see what this "Artisan Co-Op" was all about. The large metal sign stretched across the 50s-style brown brick building read "Moonbeam Mountain Coffeehouse"

Inside of the building could be called politely a cacophony of kitsch memorabilia. Posters, stickers, small trinkets like worry stones, gemstones, to larger table pieces like giant gemstone spheres, obelisks, and paintings. I grabbed a coffee from the counter and started to peruse. I owed my dad about 15 years worth of birthday presents anyway, so maybe something in here would be nice for his office. Something I didn't know what how well the scent of freshly brewed coffee would blend with cedarwood, patchouli and incense, creating an odd heady aroma that was as comforting as it was grounding. I'd been awake for about 10 hours, and felt foolish feeling run down, however in my defense, it'd been a hell of a 10 hours. A soft hum of folk music drifted from a corner where a local guitarist strummed and sang, her voice blending into the chatter of patrons scattered around mismatched tables and chairs. Each table seemed to have its own personality: some were bare wood, scarred from years of use, while others were painted with intricate designs, one featuring what looked like a hand-painted mural of the mountains at sunset.

I sipped the coffee—a rich, dark roast with a hint of something floral I couldn't quite place—and wandered past shelves crammed with treasures. Worry stones smoothed by countless hands caught the light, reflecting colors from a nearby stained-glass lamp. A sign read: "Locally Crafted by the Hands of the Appalachians" in wobbly, hand-painted script, and a piece of slate leaned against the shelf listing the artists’ names.

Further in, the shelves transitioned to larger items: gemstone obelisks that glittered like they held secrets, twisted driftwood sculptures, and paintings of ethereal landscapes. One caught my eye: a misty forest rendered in shades of green and gold, light pouring in like it was alive. The signature at the bottom was just two initials, the letters curling like vines.

My gaze snagged on a decorative skull perched on a tall wooden stand in the back corner. It was carved with strange, geometric designs and inlaid with a striking amethyst disc in the center of the forehead. My coffee halfway to my mouth, I paused, drawn toward it. It wasn’t just the artistry—though it was stunning—but something about it felt… familiar, though I’d never seen it before in my life.

Perfect, I thought. For Dad’s office. Definitely weird enough to get a comment, even from him.

I got the attention of the clerk in house, and had the case unlocked, the clerk gently passing me the skull to take to the counter at the front of the shop. I gasped as I took the skull in my arms, feeling a tingle of electric current almost coming from it. "Oh. That's wild." I said to no one in particular, moving to the desk and setting the skull down for purchase.

That accomplish, and the skull safely wrapped in newspaper and placed in a plastic bag I started back towards my fathers with my refilled coffee and new prize in tow.
