II PROTECT?

November 19, 2025 Act 1 Chapter 2 Scene 1 draft 2092 words

"Is this-" I prompted.

"Yep. The photographs that we took from the scene, some initial ideas of what might have transpired when." Ryan's voice took on a monotonous, hollow tone, one familiar to me of someone who'd seen a whole lot of violence over a very small window of time. I flipped open the folder and began riffing through the photographs, and I could see why.

The gore was intense. Intense might have been a mild word for what had happened to these people. Severed arm, a few torn throats, crushed bones, crushed torso and smashed skulls, and a head cleared clean from its body. A notable image I stopped at, pretty redhead in a blood-soaked cream knit sweater, her throat separated, and her eyes staring blankly to the left of frame over a mess of cascading, tangled hair caused my stomach to drop. My eyes narrowed looking at the close up photos of the wound, "Ryan, this wound here."

He glanced over to see what photo I was on as he started to accelerate up the winding mountain highway. "Ah. yeah. Pretty clean for claws or teeth huh?" His tone was knowing, a gentle lilt hidden in it, prodding me to keep looking. I obliged, curious myself, and seeing what he was probably referring to: A photograph of words written in a dark crimson in the dust of the trail "II PROTECT?". I felt my eyes turn to dinner plates. I looked out of the window in thought. She did mean something by writing that, but I had no way to know what, also what were the first two characters exactly? She'd done the rest of the word in a, for what she was going through, very clean and distinct all caps, so If they were 'L's they'd have the bend. So my money was on two 'I's. But why? Like Roman numerals? What would that have to do with anything?

"One of the victims or an assailant?" I asked aloud.

"Victim. The linguistics prodigy Doctor Margaret Jones. Apparently in for a nice tour from Ireland itself." His tone was neutral, but the forced calm made me think that he was shaken by the young lady's death. "She had a mixture of dirt and blood on her fingertips. Apparently literally held her throat together long enough to leave a message. Hence the additional craziness.”

"What does this mean, is this referencing some sort of group in the area?"

“Not that I’m aware of. Stratton’s pretty small to be running a protection racket.” Ryan said dismissively, prompting me to move on in the questions

"Are the Feds there?" I asked, still looking at the photo of Dr. Jones, and noting the sad irony that she'd never have an adventurous life like her incidental fictional namesake. That was at least the train of thought I chose to focus on under the direct questions with Ryan, since the rest of my brain screamed about the implications this had with my father's fate. Was he off the trail and away from the group when the attack had happened? If not, why was he the only one not present? More questions, less answers.

"... so between the guns, the cryptic message written in blood, the federal land and what all, I called the Forestry Service. They said they sent the Law Enforcement and Investigation unit, but they never showed. Called the FBI in Lincoln City- same thing. Then we got a count of bodies, and called the SBI while we waited, since there's no way Morrison down in Jewel Hill will handle all of this by himself, so SBI is sending some people up as well but they weren't there when I left. So we called Sam Evans over in Jewel Hill- he’s the Chief of Police over there- and had him and his boys and some more guys from over in Whisper Point come help take pictures and collect parts to take to the morgue." Ryan finished.

I blinked as I noticed something out of the window. "Uh. Didn't you say the bodies were down in Jewel Hill?"

"Mmmmhmmm." Ryan hummed pleasantly, as if I had asked if the sky were blue.

"So... we aren't going there?" My tone was confused, but laced with the beginnings of suspicion. This was going to be like the notebook all over again.

"Nope. Bigger fish first. Then we can start in on the mystery of it."

Black-meet-mail. "Oh no. Come on. We don't have time for-"

"GODDAMNIT JUDAH." Ryan roared. The tension in the cab jumped suddenly to be comparable to the pressure around the Titanic, and I felt an overwhelming dryness in my mouth. I noticed that I could feel the cool temperature of the mountain air outside against my shoulder, where I had jumped and pushed away from the outburst, not expecting it out of him.

He then went quiet and cleared his throat. "Sorry. But. We're going to town, and you are going to get settled. There's an order that this should be done, because I, for one, am not letting your skinny ass skitter away as fast as you can just because you think you've solved the puzzle. We are going to be opening a lot of wounds for everyone involved, us included, and you are going to be an ADULT MAN and deal with it."

I, as a rule, am a person who does not "Deal with things". Things happen, or they don't, things that have happened, have happened, and there's nothing to do but press forward. But that's only half a mind of how I am, because in the dark corners, I'm raging. I'm screaming, and I'm not letting that come to the surface to flail in futility. "I-" With a false start, I tried again, swallowing against the dry mouth, as the pressure of the atmosphere of the car felt like a warm custard that you could cut with a knife. "I'm willing to try?" I managed, which is more commitment than anyone had gotten out of me outside of the Navy for working with my family dynamic.

"You'd better." Ryan, as I had said previously, was not someone of intimidating stature, and normally not one of intimidating demeanor. However, his energy was frightening in such a small space. He had taken this as a life mission that I was going to see through a family reconnection, good or bad, to the end. I harrumphed and settled back into the seat. "Yes, dad." I said finally, attempting to relieve some of the tension.

"If I was Dad I'd already have beat you with the belt."

"Oooh, Daddy promise?" I said playfully, and that time he did start going for the belt.

After several minutes more minutes of driving, Ryan and I bumped over the hill and through a train intersection, the familiar brownstone, two-story brick buildings of the main street coming into view, with the cardboard cutouts of various nares-do-wells pasted on the inside of the upper windows to show that it was both a jail and police station. The building stretched down the main road, and off the side road, creating an impenetrable wall of town administration and capitalism right as you came off the bridge over the French Broad. We passed the intersection, the buildings standing over us as silent, stoic sentinels that'd seen the town through the better part of a century, and where I'd anticipated Ryan to pull off and stop at the police station, it was conveniently on the corner as you entered town, he continued down the strip, revealing even more changes, revealing a town I had so much connection to, but barely even knew at this point. My muscles started to tense and ache, as a desire to get out of the car increased, and I started to eye Ryan suspiciously as we turned right, starting up the highway again, that would eventually lead to Newport, but I knew we weren't going there. "So, do not pass go-" I said, realizing that he meant to drop me off at the one place I wanted to be least in the world with this situation, my father's.

"Do not collect $200." He nodded. "I was given specific instructions this morning that you're to be dropped off at Isaac's and'll be staying there." The glint in his eye betrayed his amusement at my predicament. Staying at my father's meant staying with Temperance, who in addition to being his live-in research assistant was my also-apparently-angry-at-me sister-in-law. I also had a suspicion on who might have given these "specific" instructions.

I swallowed again, remembering the terror that Temper Bennett could be. I felt the tension begin to run up my back as I became increasingly uncomfortable with this idea of 'jumping in feet first'. "I don't think that's a good idea. You know how she gets and-"

"-and there's a lot of reason why someone might be angry with you after 20 years of up and disappearing. I'm glad you see her line of thought." The bastard was practically merry about what was happening here. Cruelty.

He must have read my face and growing irritation out of the corner of his eye, because he sobered steadily and said "Look. I get that you don't want to get involved in it. It's done, it's too late to do anything about it. You've always been like that, even when we was kids. However, She'll know probably the most about what was going on with Isaac up to him taking this trip, and the sooner you two make nice the sooner she'll be less of a pain to be around while we all-" He emphasized. "-all try to get your dad back in one piece and try to figure out what happened up there. So it's time to pull up the big boy pants and deal."

"Aafo." I said in a deadpan. He was right and I didn't care for it.

"Bless you?" Ryan said, a curious note to his tone.

"It's Somalian for "Calamity". It's just kind of hung around since I was stationed in Bahrain." I said glumly, looking at the passing structures and mountains and not making eye contact with Ryan.

"Well isn't that Temper to a tee. Besides, she's expecting you, so if we don't show up, she'll find us anyway." Ryan said as we crunched into the gravel of my father's driveway, the tires crushing the rocks into each other, grinding them away much like my hopes for a remotely peaceful morning to reflect on the information that I'd amassed since getting off the plane. The Bronco swayed ever so slightly on the uneven surface as Ryan locked it into park and killed the ignition.  I raised an eyebrow to him and he held the keys outstretched to me. "I'll use the Jeep while you are here. Just... try not to wreck the old girl. I've had it for a long time, you know."

I sighed. "Yeah, I guess it has been a long time huh?" Everyone was convinced then that it wasn't a good fit for Ryan, but then again at the time everyone was convinced that I was a great fit for him despite our mutual protests. Funny how things work out. Ryan slid out of the seat and stretched, and I did the same, taking in a lung full of the now, even more crisp mountain air. We'd properly transcended into the smokies coming from Cedarville, and now, even at noon there was a sharpness to the chill in the air.

I stopped and looked at my father's home. It'd been built by his grandfather and father who'd ordered it from a Sears catalog. Back I guess when you could order such a thing like that from a big box store. It'd seen some changes since I'd left the last time, New exterior, new roof, solar panels, but on the inside, in the bones, it remained the same old place, ordered from that same catalog, and really what did that mean for anything really changing here, outside of the superficial?

"Well, I'm off. I need to get the Jeep and then head into the office. Got to pick up the last details from what we got sent from the LEI this morning, and then I'll come back and maybe save you if Temperance isn't done with you by then." Ryan laughed at the thought and turned, heading down the road towards his house, whistling like he hadn't a care in the world. Lucky bastard.
