The Too-Early Morning After

“Temperance. Are you ok?” I hoped that wasn’t as shaky sounding as I felt. Cause I was really out on a limb here. “Judah? WHY THE FUCK—” She started, her temper launching off. I pointed at her arm, where the blackened veins were still visible. “What?” she rubbed at the arm. “There’s no way I slept through you doing something as childish as tracing my veins—” “Fucking really? I would have just gone to bed over that Temp.” I complained stepping back, backing myself against her bedroom wall and sinking to the floor before my shaking knees betrayed me and I fell on my ass. “What is with you?” She asked, bewildered. She looked at me like I was the one doing the weird thing, and not the one that had just walked in on her unconscious Welsh David Copperfield act. I held up the stone, the pink glow fading but still ember bright in the now near black of the room. “Well, I found this.” “And? I know you found it. It fell out of your pocket when you were asleep in the recliner. I put it on the coffee table next to your sandwich that you’d fallen asleep before you could eat.” “It wasn’t next to the sandwich.” I pointed at the door. “I woke up, ate the sandwich, started to go to the study, and saw a light coming from under your door—” “And you didn’t think “Oh, it’s just a nightlight?”” “Literally my first thought. That you had the creepiest pink oscillating night light that I’d ever seen.” I shook my head ruefully. “But that didn’t explain why I could see it from the recliner, and also you don’t have a pink oscillating night light.” She pursed her lips. She wanted to argue. Not that I could see her in the dark yet, but That’s what I imagined was happening. “Well, I mean—” “Anyway, so I knock, and am like “temperance?” and open the door and find you doing the absolute hardest Gozer cosplay. Floating over the bed, hair everywhere like you sleep in a fish tank, this little fucker,” I gestured with the stone “doing it’s best impression of a glowstick, and your arm veins going black. So I grabbed it from you.” She stared at me and was very still. Ominously still. “Bullshit.” “I’d have thought so had I not literally just had it happen to me.” “Bullshit. You are just trying to freak me out now.” A whisper. “I mean, it’d make it a party of two, cause I’m pretty fucking freaked myself.” I stared at the stone. “you were—” “Don’t. I don’t want to hear any more. Get out of my room Judah.” “Awake O Sleeper.” I said. Not really paying attention to her, trying to get my bearings. Why did I know that’s what she said? “…The time has come for the healing word.” she echoed. Her breathing got shakey and even in the dark I could see her trembling. “The spirit who crossed your path—” “—is now bound.” I finished. “Judah” Her voice was a plea, a tearful plea. “Why did I know that?” I looked down at the stone in my hand, The ember of pink light fading out. “I don’t know Temper, but it can’t be good.”

Ryan yawned as he sat at the dining room table. “So, let me get this straight.” he folded his hands together and pointed them at me. “You, wake up, see a light, start to go down the hallway. The light can’t possibly be hitting where you saw it.” “For the twenty third time now, yes.” I said flatly, taking a sip from a mug of coffee I’d made after getting Temperance to come off the bed and out into the living room. She was holding her own mug with both hands, knees tucked to her chest, wrapped in a knit throw that’d come from Great-Grandma Sands. “You see the light coming from under Temperance’s bedroom door, You open it, and you see her—” “—floating. Yes.” “With venous discoloration. I can attest to that. it was still visible when I got here.” Anna chimed in. She was standing across the coffee table from Temperance, trying to not directly watch her, but also definitely watching her. Her face was etched in a look of pure concern and absolute no bullshit business. When I’d called her earlier, apologizing profusely for waking her, and tried to sketch the situation she’d almost hung up on me. Then I’d sent her a photo of Temperance’s arm. She’d appeared minutes later and did a battery of at home tests on her, checking her for bruises, pupil dilation responses, anything it seemed like someone trained would think of. “And her eyes were glowing.” Ryan said, looking back at me. “Yep. Green.” “Like, what are we talking about? Like her eyes were just bright? She’s got green eyes.” I looked at him sourly. “No, more green than normal. Like not Nuclear goo green, but like—” “If you shined a flashlight through an emerald.” Temperance whispered, her voice shaking. Anna narrowed her eyes at the meek sound. It was very not Temperance. “And she was whispering.” Ryan said, slowly, concern also etched into his face as he watched Temperance take a small sip from her mug. “Yes. In Welsh.” “Do you know Welsh?” “No. But for some reason I know it was Welsh.” “And she was saying something about binding?” Temperance slowly turned her head to him. “Ryan. It was practically a spell. I don’t remember saying it. I don’t remember any of this, just waking up and finding Judah in my room holding that— thing.” she tilted her mug at the garnet. Sitting in the middle of the coffee table, under the carnival glass candy dish lid. it’s own little quarantine. “Though the blood is not pure, the pact is bound. Awaken O sleeper. The time has come for the healing word. The spirit that crossed your path is now bound.” She shrunk further into the throw. “Whatever the fuck that means.” Ryan paused and considered. “And you said all that in Welsh?” “Apparently I whispered it creepily according to Captain Jackass.” “Temperance,” his tone flat and directive, chiding Temperance for name calling. “Ryan.” I said softly. “It’s the stone that was in the journal. I guess it was in the journal when I tagged it? Maybe fell out into my pocket when I pulled the journal out and discovered the hole?” “That’s evidence?” Ryan asked incredulously. “I mean, yeah.” “Under the candy dish lid.” “I mean, what was I supposed to do after all that, throw it in a ziplock?” “I don’t think anyone should be touching it at all.” Anna said flatly. “I’ve never seen discoloration like that before. Temperance appears to be physically fine, but that’s… horrifying. Even ignoring the other weirdness.” An electronic rooster crowed from her pocket. “That’s my alarm for Abraham. I need to get up to his place and check in on him.” She grabbed her bag from beside the door and looked back at Temperance. “If you—” she looked at me “—either of you, see any changes in the other or yourself as far as physical responses, you let me know immediately. I don’t know what that stone is, or where it came from, but that was more than just an allergic reaction to something and we have to watch.” We gave the obligatory “Yes ma’am.” as she pushed open the door and closed it behind her.

Temperance excused herself to her room a few minutes after Anna’s exit, and left me and Ryan in the living room. “Judah, I don’t know what to make of any of this.” Ryan said hollowly. “How do you think I feel? You’re just listening to the crazy person story, not being the crazy person.” my shoulders sagged, everything felt too heavy, and it wasn’t just the exhaustion. “So—” Ryan started “So?” “Your Dad had a drawing of this in his journal. And this in his journal.” Ryan pulled out his phone, assumably to pull up the pictures of the journal pages I’d taken the night before. “I’m not sitting down around a tiny ass phone screen and doing this.” I said flatly, standing up. “Lets go to the study, I’ll pull it up over there and we can look on the monitor.” We got back to the office, past Tyler’s room where the kid had surprisingly slept through everything that had happened overnight, and entered Dad’s Study. He’d added it on to the house after I’d left, wall to ceiling bookshelves, large double pane windows with blackout curtains, a large wooden executive style desk that he could have easily brought back home with him when he retired as a professor. On the desk was a 32in TV pretending to be a monitor, and a button labelled “Wall Screen”. Well color me curious. I pressed it. A near-belching roar of what I could only assume was a steam powered cooling fan kicked on, and a projector on one of the bookcases roared to life, the bulb flickering on and casting a yellowish tinted white light across the room. A screen automatically slid down into position in response, and the lights I had flicked on upon entering dimmed. Ok Dad. Neat trick. “Oh, he comes to my house to watch the Superbowl and he had THIS?” Ryan said in less than mock offense. “You’re telling me. Never came to my place for the Superbowl. Or told me he had this either.” I sat down at the desk and Ryan sat in an armchair in front of the projector. I clicked the sign in and was met with a lock screen asking for a password. “P-A-S-S-W-O-R-D” I said as I typed. “Really?” Ryan said giving me a look, and then groaning as the computer’s desktop loaded and started populating icons. I mirrored the screens so we could both see the same thing, and started browsing the icons. “Sandman of Mott’s Creek”, “Devils Stamping Ground”, “Congress of Cedars”, “Whispers of Lover’s Leap”, “The Grey Shepard”, “Corven”. I clicked on Corven. That name sounded familiar. Inside there was a saved email chain between Dad and someone with the email j.corven@corvidsnestresearch.net. Well then. “That’s not a personal address to a fan.” Ryan shifted in the armchair. “I mean, anyone can buy a domain these days, but—” I opened the email chain. Ryan whistled. “That’s a lot of zeros.” I was stunned. No wonder Dad had taken that expedition. He’d thrown a seven digit number at Corven and Corven basically said “That’s fine, here’s who else is coming.” I’m pretty sure Dad’s whole social media cryptozoological “enterprise” was worth well less than what he demanded from James Corven. “I have it from various sourcing that the encampment of the Madocian Druids would be somewhere near you, and I think you know roughly where I’m talking about.” Corven had told Dad. “I’ve done some looking at the Congress of Cedar rumors, although Madog ab Owain Gwynedd is a few centuries later than their attested arrival. I’ve not really seen any evidence of truth to any of it.” I clicked the attached document with “whom to expect” as the title. “Oh boy. Judah.” Ryan said, scanning the email. “Yep. Nageshi, Petrovya, Ramirez, and Jones.” I looked around the TV at my friend. “So I guess we know who funded the expedition now.” “And the list of contractors.” Ryan said absently. I forwarded him the email so he could document it, and opened the Congress of Cedar folder. Several text documents, pdfs, maps, and one lone domino's receipt that was printed to pdf but probably saved in the wrong folder. I attached them to an email and also sent them to Ryan. I’d look at those later. I opened a web browser and logged into my email, where I had sent the photos of the journal last night. I also opened a second tab and typed ‘James Corven’ into the search. James Corven was apparently minorly famous. Corvid’s Nest Research was apparently an arm of Corvid’s Nest Media, which produced a Web TV show called “Dr. Crypto’s Mysteries”, that featured Corven and a list of other researchers and would cover topics as wide from Ancient Aliens, to lost cultures, to Bigfoot and a special Mothman episode. “Ok, so Cryptid Alex Jones asked your Dad to—?” “I guess pre-vet the Congress of Cedar to see if it was enough to make an episode about.” Seemed like strained logic considering the price tag for just my Dad, but that was the most obvious conclusion.

I sat with that thought for a second that didn’t seem long enough when the computer speakers beeped a happy note, blossoming a notification from Dad’s email client on the desktop.

Ryan looked up from his phone and shifted forward in the chair. “Hey, isn’t that?”

The dialogue box indicated the email was from Corvid’s Nest Research. I clicked it to open the email, and froze.

It had been sent to my dad, but more than that it was CC’d with a trio of emails. levi.sands@gardencreekcounselling.org, Tsands@sandsresearch.com, and my own personal email. My eyes narrowed. One, Corven or someone employed by him was hunting for dad. That much was obvious, why email all of his kids outside of not expecting him to access his own emails. Second, Dad didn’t have that email for me. Corven appeared to though.

“Looking for you. Heard there was trouble at the trail. Don’t speak to police until one of our suits gets there. Respond back ASAP.”

Well then. Ryan snorted audibly. “Well, if we didn’t want to talk to him before, definitely would after that email.” Not incorrect. “The question I have is why send it to all us kids too. That seems… sloppy if you are trying to hide something. A lot of eyes.” “That is assuming he wanted it to not be seen.” Ryan said. “I think this is more a preemptive back off than an offer of legal assistance.” I frowned. Curious, I clicked into the inbox of the email client and looked in the sent folder. “Wait—” Ryan started reading along on the projector “Yep. That saved email phoned home. Someone back at the nest saw it accessed.” “And emailed Temperance and Levi too.” “What’s Garden Creek Counselling?” I asked, peering around the TV at my friend. Ryan started to open his mouth to explain, but the sound caught in his throat, he looked around perplexed, like he was trying to locate something. “Do you hear that?” I looked down at the desk. It was ringing? I opened the tray drawer, but there was nothing there. I could feel the vibrations in the wood from something. I started to close it back, but noticed a light on my jeans travel with it, and pawing the bottom of the tray discovered a small flip phone inside a pouch attached under the drawer. The small screen was lit with a number I didn’t recognize, and it wasn’t displaying a contact name What the hell dad. I looked up blankly at Ryan. “Well answer it?” He said, pulling out his phone and mouthing “on speaker.” I flipped open the phone and hit the green key. “May I assume I’m speaking to one of the Misters Sands?” I looked at Ryan who gave me a thumbs up. “You can assume that.” I said flatly. “Who am I speaking to?” “Well, based on the clipped tone, I’d assume that I’m speaking to the elder brother Judah.” the electronic and tinny speaker drolled. “My name is Kevin Corven, one of the founders of Corven Media, and we’d heard that your father had had an incident on his way back from an expedition we commissioned.”
