The Fall of the Father

December 30, 2025 Act 3 Chapter 44 Scene 2 draft 901 words

Judah Sands Isaac Sands Levi Sands Ryan Thompson George Walton Arthmon (Garnet - Protection) Abraham Sands Tyler Sands Dylan Morgan

I’ve heard less chaotic war zones. But then again, most of the action I’ve seen was either open air or in a building that had furniture and interior walls that dampened sound. A mostly empty stone cavern is a nightmare for awareness—every shot amplified, repeated, echoed, and redirected. Every scream or shout distorted. From our hiding spot behind the altar, it was almost impossible to tell who was winning in the gun battle versus the monster that was my grandfather, outside of the fact that it was still happening, which gave the betting odds to Arthmon.

“Our… birthright?” Levi whisper-shouted over the noise, his attention mostly focused on checking his boys for injuries himself now that he was in cover.

“Best I can word it,” I said, before we hunkered again, another spray of pebbles cascading over the edge of the stone behind us. “Short version? Druids, magic, spirits that get passed down bloodlines—” and I jerked a thumb back toward the chaos “—and Pa.”

Levi just stared at me. “That’s. Insane.”

I didn’t disagree. I pushed myself sideways to peek around the altar. Arthmon had pushed Walton and company farther down the path they were on, closer to the cavern’s main entrance. I didn’t think it was quite a selfless act of protection for us, angling them away from us so the crossfire wouldn’t chew through the altar, but more that Arthmon didn’t want his prizes killed before he could transfer himself from my dying grandfather and continue the bloodline.

I looked at the other side of the room and froze.

“Oh God.”

Isaac Sands—the cryptozoologist, heir of Arthmon, and our father—sat against the wall of the cavern, three dark red circles in his shirt, the glossy wet stains spreading, glare twinkling from the firelight around him in the illuminated chamber.

The chaotic screams of the fight in the cavern stilled as my mind went blank. I was moving before I’d registered that I’d left the safety of our hiding spot, barely registering the tug of my shirt as Ryan and Levi both grabbed for me. I sprinted, the aches and searing pain of my ribs gone as I slid across the stone floor down to my father.

“No no no no no—Dad.”

Another sound of scraping and Levi was next to me, grabbing my father’s arm. “JUDAH, WE GOTTA MOVE!” he shouted to me, my mind barely processing it above the dissociative ring of terror and horror at my father’s condition.

Instinct and training took over then. We couldn’t stay out here; that was asking to get shot too. I looped my father’s other arm over my shoulder and, with Levi, we ran him to the altar, laying him down behind it.

Small favors were that whatever Arthmon had done to Tyler and Dylan, they were still unconscious and didn’t have to see their grandfather like this. Dad’s eyes were glassy, and he stared at the ceiling. The only sign he was still there was the ragged rise and fall of his bullet-ravaged chest, and the pump of blood from the three wide holes in his chest.

The only thing I could hear was the caustic boil of my blood in my ears. There were no tears; I couldn’t afford them, and I was damned for it. Levi was busy at work, desperately ripping off his over shirt and attempting to plug and apply pressure to the wounds as skillfully as someone that had binged a medical procedural far too much. It was too much. Too bad. With being out here with Arthmon for weeks? There was a fatalism to thinking about it, and right now my brain was refusing to do it, cheering my brother on.

I stood—far more gracefully than I had been managing—spun, and fired, the gun kicking in my hand, muzzle flash lighting the dust, dropping one of the khaki-clad assailants as Arthmon pressured them with powerful swipes and roars. I gave a roar of my own before I was suddenly jerked back down by my collar. Ryan was inches away, looking me eye to eye, and taking the gun from me. He glanced down at Isaac to draw my attention.

Levi was kneeling over our father, grasping one of his hands. I kneeled down as my father pawed the ground around me, lacing my fingers in his. One squeeze. Two squeezes. Tears rolled down my father’s face as he locked on to first me, and then Levi. The third squeeze faltered, the fingers spasming halfway through the motion.

His chest collapsed under its own weight, no longer strong enough to rise again.

The exhale was ragged and final. A small smile crossed my father’s lips as he moved his eyes finally to the cavern ceiling, and then he stopped moving, the hand going limp in mine. The warm, blood-slicked finger twitched once against my palm.

The cavern forgot to echo—or I forgot to hear it. The temperature around me dropped as quickly as my heart in that moment, watching the dust and movement of my father settle into the release of oblivion.

From behind us, a raw howl of anguish rose from the monster’s throat. Inside the monster, inside that corrupted form, I could almost hear Abraham—grieving through the creature that had devoured him.
