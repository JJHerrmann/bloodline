Click, Click, Boom

December 29, 2025 Act 3 Chapter 43 Scene 2 draft 604 words

Please leave comments on the scene using the form at the end of this section.

The door swung open with a long creak, betraying its own age and the lack of mechanical care that had come to the house during my grandfather’s illness and my father’s disappearance. I’d have to get on that. The house was unnaturally dark, even with the lights off. It seemed like the house was swallowing every source of light in the room, from the medical machine monitors, the moonlight through the windows, anything. Then my mind registered the sound. Several medical machines calling out for assistance, saying they didn’t detect life in their charge. Alarmed, my hand went to the wall to turn on the lights of the room as I stepped inside. I felt the slightest resistance to my footfall. My stomach dropped. Tripwire. In the darkness, in response to my step, the face of a cellphone lit up, casting a strong rim-light under the nightstand where it had been stowed, attached to something. An uncomfortably large something. I launched myself back into the doorframe, blocking the doorway and wrapping Anna in my arms. Light. Thunder. My back exploded into a thousand lancets of pain. I wasn’t sure how much was the heat of the explosion cooking my back through my jacket, or if it was pieces of house and medical equipment slamming into me. “Abe!” I heard Anna sob from my shoulder as I was pitched forward, veering dangerously close to the edge of the deck, and then over the railing as the darkness edged on my vision.

My inner ear screamed as we pitched ass over kettle over the railing, launching my stomach into somersaults. I shifted my weight, trying to roll and get Anna above me so that she didn’t land under me. She would not do well under me at the end the 20 feet below the deck onto a dangerous looking slope leading to the low road below next to the river.

I only partially managed, landing on my shoulder opposite Anna’s head and beginning to slide and roll. Leaves, sticks, small saplings cracked past my ears as we began to slide and tumble down the hill side, my back and shoulder crashing into exposed rock, cuts and welts striping my neck and shoulders as we went. with a final sickening thud, we finally stopped moving, my back against a tree trunk looking up the hill at my grandfather’s home., Anna cradled in my arms, unconscious. I just had to hope she hadn't hit her head. I think I had managed to keep her from harm.

Fire erupted from the roof of the house, out the south wall, and I could see flames spreading from the floor underneath the decking. My mind was fogged, the pain in my back from hitting the tree, the pain in my shoulders and knees from the fall. I just sat, and breathed. My breath came in ragged, smoky gasps against the night air.

I heard the sirens. Good. Someone had reported the fire.

My vision fluttered. This was not a good sign.

Was the roar in my ears closer? I looked around confused and froze when I craned my head even higher into the tree above us.

Hawk talons. Behind those talons, muscular arms flexed under moonlit scales, and behind that a heaving, concave chest, like a starving bear. It had a deer’s skull on it’s face, complete with antlers, and underneath looked like a rabid coyote. The eyes practically glowed with the purplish maroon of garnet, as it peered down at me from it’s perch.

Arthmon.

Well, at least Pa is alive. I thought, as the world went black around me.

Re-cairn-ation

December 29, 2025 Act 3 Chapter 43 Scene 3 draft 724 words

Please leave comments on the scene using the form at the end of this section.

“Anna!” I roared, launching up from a sitting position. “Anna?” Instead of finding myself on the forested slope of the bluff next to my grandfather’s house, I found myself in a stone cave. Stalagmites and stalactites created a dark parody of teeth around the chamber, some lined up almost perfectly, creating a motif of pillars throughout the room. I was surrounded by the flickering fires of candlelight and braziers in the floor, copper, hand-hammered, at a guess. The ritual cave? From the journal? Suddenly my vision blurred and I lost my sense of balance as the pain from the explosion and landing suddenly reminded my body of how much it had taken in that fall. I was in rough shape. I felt a hand on my shoulder steady me. “Easy boy. I don’t know what you got into, but it definitely messed you right up.” If it weren’t for the injury already causing a lack of control of some of my muscles, I’d have gone slack jawed. “Dad?!” I whirled around and hugged him. “Holy shit. I’ve been– I’ve been looking for you all over this mountain. Where?–” He held up a hand. “We’re in the cavern. Right where it wants us to be.” His features darkened in the candlelight. “I’m sorry son. I tried to get him to take me, but he thinks I’m too old. I’m a risk for him to transfer to. He’s afraid I’ll try to end the line just like Pa.” “Tried to get who–” Then I saw them. Tyler and Dylan lying on what I can only describe as being a stone altar near the back of the cavern. “No.” My dad nodded. “Yeah, that’s what I said too. But Arthmon? He’s got hisself a plan. Start young, be the experienced voice of reason in the boys heads. Let him be in control instead of controlled by the human host.” “How do you?” “Know? Because the spirit talks, I listen. Mostly from lack of other things to do. Unlike Beauty and the Beast, the thing didn’t build me a library or anything to spend my time.” I groaned at the allegory, but I allowed it. “So.” I dropped my hands to my sides. I was so tired. “What can we even do about it?” “Well,” my father said, clicking his tongue behind his teeth and looking around the room. “We’ve got the garnet separated. So there’s not much he can do as far as the transfer. I’ve–” I thought about the leather looped around my neck, with the garnet plinth tied to it. Best not to mention it. The less anyone knew, the less anyone could tell Arthmon. “So, he just left you alone down here? Have you tried to escape?” My dad scoffed and rolled his eyes. “To what effect, boy? The damn thing lives just up the hill from me. It’d be only a matter of time before he hoodwinked me and brought me back, and no telling what he’d do to Tyler or Temperance tryin’ t’do it.” I flinched, “How is this even a thing? Love him, but Pa–” “Yeah, Pa is all but gone, and had almost taken that thing with him. I–” My dad sighed and looked to the cavern ceiling. “I just couldn’t let him go. Spent all that money to eke every last minute with him I could. I didn’t–” “Dad. Literally no way to have known that you were allowing any sort of problem to perpetuate. We didn’t know about Arthmon, the gemstone, the bloodline, any of it. I didn’t know until you told me, you and that Maggie lady.” “Maggie?” My dad looked perplexed. “How– he sliced her throat clean open and knocked me ass over teakettle down the side of the ridge. How did–” “To be honest, I’m not sure. But, she… she’s been explaining things. Like I thought it was mom at first, like being here was doing something to my brain, giving me dreams. But then she started explaining some of the gaps in your notes.” “Shit.” My dad said flatly. “I mean, it’s a little weird and–” “Velandor.” Dad said, flatly– almost ominously. I froze. The candles flickered, and a cold wash rand down my body. “How did you?–” “You’ve been reading my journal,” he said. “I didn’t get a chance to write everything down.”
