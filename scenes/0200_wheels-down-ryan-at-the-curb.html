Wheels Down; Ryan at the Curb

November 19, 2025 Act 1 Chapter 2 Scene 0 draft 710 words

Honestly, I've had worse experiences at airports. TSA checked my hardcase, and my duffel, but as soon as they saw the ID, they backed off for the most part from the most invasive of search routines. I took a medium flight from Norfolk down to Atlanta, and then hopped a puddle jumper to come back up and land at Crowsburg, which is always a terrifying experience, hitting the patches of turbulence as the wet wild winds of the Midwest crest the Appalachians and jump the rain shadow below, crashing down in a tsunami of wet air ready to dampen the more amenable regions.

With a new appreciation for life and being on the ground, I strode out the doors of the Crowsburg airport, donning a pair of sunglasses, now that it was late enough for that to be a need with the sun high in the sky. I'd made good time, from getting my ticket right around 4:30, to landing at 11:30.

I looked around and saw an all too familiar sight that I was impressed still existed. A Ford Bronco 79 was sitting on the asphalt of the pick up lane, with an impressively preserved paint job, Forest green sinking into the warm gradients of red, orange and yellow in the wide stripes down both sides of the truck, its chrome grill polished as the day it rolled off the lot. Next to it, blowing into his hands as he took in the sights of the traveling back and forth was Ryan Thompson. My best friend since childhood, and singular officer of the Deep Springs Police Department for the last 10 years, since Joe Miller retired back in 2014. He was decked in a T Shirt and jeans, with a flannel casually worn open on his thin frame. He was not a very tall or imposing presence, standing 5'5" and weighing in at 150ish pounds, but I knew him to be as tough as any of the 6ft shipmates I'd ever served with, and he gave a doozy. Ryan lifted a palm upwards as he caught sight of me, a duffle bag slung over my shoulder with my hardcase tucked away inside of it.

"Hey buddy, talk about turn around times. Luckily the flights worked out in my favor, I just got away long enough ago to get down here."

Deep Springs and the scene were about 45 minutes northwest of here, and I suspected that Ryan was being generous on his estimation of not having to wait for me. I opened the door of the old Bronco and felt greeted as if by an old friend. As obscenely garish and green as the truck exterior was, its interior gave a good fight for top dog. Green carpets and wood paneling stretched across the flat dashboard, and a meticulously cared for green bench seat stretched across both driver and passenger sides of the vehicle, with a band of patterned green cloth in the center where the original manufacturer expected occupants. We'd spent many days riding this monster between Tennessee, Crowsburg, and home. I reached over the seat and tossed my duffel in the back, as Ryan clambered into the driver's side. I noticed a manilla folder sitting in the passenger's seat, grabbing it up as I slid in, shut the door and fastened my seatbelt. "So. How bad is it still up there?"

"Well right before I left, they had carted the last of the bodies off the trail and sent them on the way to Jewel Hill to get autopsies done-" Typical with animal attacks, but it also made sense with some of the unknowns of what Ryan had told me. They would want, depending on whatever agencies were already involved in the mess, to identify the animal that they thought might be responsible for the attack for sure, which means tooth and claw identifications at the Medical Examiner's office, probably, from what Ryan described, a fair amount of shell casing identification and attempting to tracking what they were shooting at when. Definitely forming a timeline of the attack, and trying to ascertain what happened when. Ryan started the truck and started to pull away from the airport terminal, and I looked at the folder in my lap.
