Night on Rich Mountain

November 19, 2025 Act 1 Chapter 4 Scene 1 draft 636 words

Ryan and I left the diner and slowly made our way in the Bronco to the summit of Rich Mountain where the fire tower loomed, keeping a vigilante gaze over the valley and surrounding mountainsides. The closest point by car to the scene of the attack on my father’s expedition, it seemed the logical place to go in order to get our own closer look. The mountain air was crisp as it wafted in through the windows of the Bronco, itself bouncing on its suspension up the dirt road that was more conducive to mountain bikes and hikers than it was a sports utility vehicle. The dirt and rocks crunched under the tires, giving off groans and pops like the ancient mountain itself had started to stir, and its joints, still for millennia, needed to let off some gas before resuming their work. As we finally crested the mountain, the sound of dirt and the occasional rock gave way to the crunch of chalky gravel under the weathered metal tower that stretched like a sentinel in the sky over the mountain top, we both noticed the other vehicle at the same time.

“The chief is here.” Ryan noted as he pulled alongside the Lincoln Continental sedan, an antique cherry police light in its dash as the only indicator of its official position as the Police Command Cruiser of the Deep Springs Police Department. It belonged to the Chief of Police, David Beech, who had been the Chief of police since I was a kid. Beech had been the one that offered me a chance to join the department, telling me that as soon as I graduated High School, and went to a police academy down in Buncombe and passed, that the job was mine to be had. Naturally, not wanting to be trapped in this town of mine, I'd refused, instead joining the Navy, but Beech had been a major motivating factor in my career as a Master at Arms, having inspired me to attempt that track with his faith in me.

“I guess he’s down at the site? I don’t see him up here?” I said as I pushed myself out of the vehicle into the crisp mountaintop air. The kind of crisp the betrayed the idea of an early snow being on its way, tinged with the slightest hint of woodsmoke from citizens in the various valleys of the area keeping low fires in wood stoves, readying them for their task of keeping homes warmer during the cold mountain nights to come.

“I suppose so.” Ryan said, the crunch of gravel that heralded his boots coming up behind me, both of us looking down the hill towards the scene of the attack, invisible in the distance through the twisted curtain of trees that wound around the footpath that had been scarred into the landscape as one of the premier hiking trails of the East Coast. “Well, I suppose let’s git and let you get your bearings.” he said, stepping past me and following the trail. Falling in step behind him, I tried to not let the sinking feeling I had about the place take root in my mind, somewhat unsuccessfully. It was a low grade panic, a sense of unease and dread that starts in your pelvis and works its way up to your lungs like honeysuckle vines and starts to choke off your breath at the root.  I’m not sure if it was the attack, Dad missing, or just my brain feeling unfamiliar with this place that it felt should be familiar to it, but something felt terribly off about the whole area. Like a hidden mist was lingering low over the ground, waiting to trap an ankle and cause an injury. Like the trail itself meant to cause harm to passers through.
