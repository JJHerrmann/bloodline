Aftermath

January 10, 2026 Act 3 Chapter 45 Scene 1 draft 605 words

After several days in the hospital I was back at home. Well, my father’s home. My heart panged a little as that thought hit my heart like a ball-ping. It wasn’t his anymore, but that didn’t stop them from relocating me here under the care of Temperance and Anna. For now I had bought myself a bit of peace, rolling out to the porch and the quiet of the bustling house, now a blend of halfway house and full home with two ten-year-old boys, Temperance, and one sore, grumpy veteran. Abraham’s house had been a total loss after the bomb that had gone off. Which tends to happen I guess. Isaac had made sure that money kept flowing in the event of his death. He’d even had a clause to make sure his “payroll” kept going, meaning Temperance wasn’t out of a job. She could research enough new content to keep Dad’s Cryptozoological blog going for a while. I sighed and put my hand against the wheel of the chair. Nothing permanent, but for now I was going to have to get used to stairs—I glanced at the road in front of the house that sloped down towards the French Broad below—and hills being my natural predators. The door popped open and Anna stepped out with two mugs, steaming in the cool winter air. “I’d say penny for your thoughts, but after what you and Temperance and Ryan told me, it might be worth a dollar or more.” “I’d trade the dollar for a coffee, I suppose.” She laughed and handed me one of the mugs. “So what’s got your sails twisted, sailor?” “Thinking about the madness of it all,” I sighed and took a sip of the hot coffee. “I spent so long running from here, and now–” I paused, struggling to find the words for the emotions I was feeling. “–and now you don’t get the chance to see your Dad for who he could have been for you,” She brushed a hand through her hair, her eyes clouding a little. ”I get it. That’s similar to when my mom died.” “Yeah.” She squatted down to eye level with me, not a stretch considering her height. “Judah, it’s ok to let that bother you. You don’t have to come out here to be a stoic sea captain when you feel like that.” I snorted and then groaned as the pain roiled through my torso. “I don’t have enough pipe for all that. All the best stoic sea captains had pipes.” “Deflection.” she chided. “Accurate,” I swilled the coffee in the cup. “It’s something that bugs me. I was so angry at him, at both my grandfathers, Levi, everyone. But now… I guess I really was mad at myself. But what could I have done? I was 10 when mom died. I couldn’t have stopped her from going, I couldn’t have stopped him from going on his expeditions to keep her from driving angry, but part of me always felt like I should have done more. Now they are both gone.” Anna smiled grimly but sincerely and placed her hand on my knee. “But not entirely,” she said after a moment. “Still got a cryptid crazy nephew that is living on for him. Get to know him.” I tilted my head. The boys were cackling inside over something on their tablet. The wind was gently blowing over the mountains into the folds of the valley, and ever so slightly I heard a blue jay’s musical call. The amethyst on my chest warmed in response, and I took Anna’s hand. “Well lets do that then.”
