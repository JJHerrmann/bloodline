We all reconvened on the trail. Beech had his arms crossed in front of him, leaning back against a tree trunk as I'd come to expect from my grandfather. Duilion and Lee thanked us for the time and for getting them up to speed then started down the trail, saying they'd report in and get in touch with Forestry. I glanced at Ryan, who, in contrast to Chief Beech, was straight as a board and had his hands wound into tight fists as we all stood in the trail wrapping up. "For all that brass—" Cooperfield said, his angered flush evaporating, replaced with a smooth, practiced prose analysis tailor-made for reporters "—the simplest answer is that your Pa hired a bunch of well-armed amateurs that couldn't hit the broad side of a—" He caught himself. "Point is, let's not make it more than it is. Tragic, but an animal attack nonetheless."

Switchbacks Confessional

November 19, 2025 Act 1 Chapter 6 Scene 2 draft 708 words

Please leave comments on the scene using the form at the end of this section.

I stared through the windshield. The trees loomed like specters in the darkness as the Bronco's headlights swept over them on the mountain switchbacks. "So, I've heard yoga helps," I said, eyeing Ryan. He grunted, "Sorry. Um. Cooperfield. He's... hard for me to be around." One of my brows arched. "I mean, I've seen he's not exactly the most lovable—" "No." His voice was a hoarse whisper. Ryan took a deep breath, his knuckles white from gripping the steering wheel, and sighed before continuing. "It's not that. Do—do you remember the incident?" "I remember it well enough to know that's underselling what happened. You were almost killed in a domestic call that went sideways." Ryan clicked his tongue softly. "Yep. That'd be the one..." "...Except," I said flatly, prompting him to continue. "except it might not have exactly..." I've had an image of that scene for the last ten years. After all, for the longest time I felt it was my fault Ryan was in that situation. In another time and world, it could have been me looking down the barrel of Duck's shotgun that night. Suddenly the image in my mind shifted, the detail that Ryan was avoiding saying clicking into place, as the tank top and gym shorts in my vision was replaced with a pressed khaki uniform. "RYAN. What the actual fuck. It's been TEN YEARS and you are just now—" “JUDAH—” Ryan said, raising his voice to interrupt my outburst. He stomped the brake to emphasize the point, causing dirt and dust to crunch and dance around the vehicle, and the belt to bite into my chest from the abrupt stop. “— I wanted to. Hell, I’d have been shouting it from every rooftop between here and Cedarville, but—” He exhaled harshly, too tight to be a laugh, but not casual enough to describe as a sigh. “—but for Ol’ Sheriff Cooperfield. He came to my hospital room not too long after I came out of surgery. Don’t think I’d even seen your dad yet. He says, ‘Now listen, son, we don’t want this unfortunate incident to blemish the public trust and make citizens feel like we can’t work together.’” I noticed that I was now the one white-knuckling. My hands were balled into tight, indignant fists. “Cooperfield what— covered it up?” “Exactly so. Not so explicitly as to make a scene over it, but enough to get the idea that my life would be even more of a mess if I went against the official report. Basically emphasized in a ‘he said’ competition, he’d back George Walton and remind people of reasons why they might not want to trust me.” We stared at each other for a few beats. Ryan wrangling with the ghosts from his confession. Me trying to reel in my own guilty feelings over the matter. “Um—” I said, glancing over to Ryan, my brain now reset. “Yeah,” Ryan said, still peering out the windshield. “Anyway. Cooperfield told me that, and the rest is history. Walton got transferred to another county, I got nearly six years of PT visits and medical bills. No therapy though, not like the town can afford the best in insurance, but still you’d think that’d be there.” I wasn’t in a place to appreciate the attempt at humor. “Ryan, If I’d have known—” “You’d have gone AWOL, fucked your career, and then gone to jail. I know.” He half-smiled. “That’s part of why I didn’t tell you, bud. At least until now. Now you can’t mess up your career path, what you do in retirement is on you I guess.” “As much as I’d be open to the idea of a jailhouse stint—” I inhaled deeply, trying to refocus. Trying to push the emotions back into the box where they needed to be instead of out and everywhere. “—Dad is on a timer. So let’s focus on that first. We find him, then we deal with Sheriff Cooperfield and Walton.” A low howl lifted from lower down the mountain. A howl as if you’d sized up a coyote to the size of a Great Dane. We both looked through the windshield, peering into the darkness around the pale cones of light the Bronco was throwing.
