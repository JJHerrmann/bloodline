The Brother

November 25, 2025 Act 2 Chapter 12 Scene 1 draft 416 words

"Judah" Sands, CPO, MA, USN (Ret.), November 25th, 2025

The clinic was easy enough to walk into, it was after all a public facility. My brother worked as a counsellor here, and once I told a cheerfully helpful looking assistant my name and asked for my brother, she was more than glad to guide me over to where he’d be. “Levi’s in a counselling right now, one of the group ones. You should be proud of him Judah, he’s a big inspiration to a lot of these folk, what has been on hard times and all.” I nodded. Truth was, the more I heard about what Levi had been doing with his life since I’d gotten back, the prouder of him I became. I may have fought battles against others, but he fought the one that I still struggled with, the battle within. The assistant, Amy, frowned as we stopped at a heavy door. She swiped her card at the sensor next to it, the LED flickered in response, shifting from red to violet, and the door not opening as she expected it to. “Another glitch?” she muttered swiping the card again, this time the LED clicking green and the door popping open obediently, refreshing her smile. “Sometimes we get our own little boogers in the works ya know.” I chuckled dryly. “That’s how it goes sometimes.” We walked several more meters across the checkered linoleum floor and Amy paused. “Now you can wait for him here” the assistant said, opening the door to a small office. “I’m sure he’ll be happy to see you,” ‘Thank you Ms. Amy. I appreciate the help’ I internally snorted at her comment. Levi’d be as happy to see me as a sheepdog to a wolf, but some things couldn’t be helped. Maybe here he’d manage a civil tone and we could discuss what I’d been learning about the “church” that backed his counselling center. I looked around the office. Books, lots of books. The Levi I knew hadn’t been an avid reader, but apparently he’d come around to it. Counselling. Therapy. Self Help. He’d really leaned into this new role. I saw a picture on the L Bend of the desk and shifted my weight forward to give it a glance. A woman with dark hair and a broad smile, holding a toddler with a mop of dark red hair who looked more elated to be on camera than in his surroundings at what looked like a trail head.
