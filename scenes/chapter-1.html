To This Point

Dr. Isaac Sands joins a research expedition to investigate Gwynporth, a rumored ancient druid settlement hidden in the North Carolina mountains. What begins as an academic dig turns dangerous fast when the team finds deliberate stonework, unnatural plantings, and a hollow oak marker inscribed in an old Latin-derived script. When Dr. Margaret Jones reads the inscription aloud, something in the land answers. The tree collapses, the ground gives way, and whatever was sleeping beneath the site wakes up.

The Call

Judah at 2 A.M.

November 19, 2025 Act 0 Chapter 1 Scene 2 draft 495 words

Please leave comments on the scene using the form at the end of this section.

"Judah" Sands, CPO, MA, USN (Ret.), November 19th, 2025

I don't love phone calls to start with. They interrupt your flow—whatever train of thought you’re riding derails the second you have to answer. And these days, the odds of it being something worthwhile? Slim. Telemarketers or scammers make sure of that.

However, I really don't like a phone call in the middle of the night. Nothing good ever comes at 2 a.m.—it’s almost always bad news.

And anyone calling me, Judah Sands, at 2 a.m. should know better — because at that hour, you’re either bringing something I don’t want to hear, or inviting me into a problem I sure as hell don’t want to be part of.

So, when my eyes snapped open in the darkness of my austere studio apartment on the Norfolk waterfront, dragged from sleep by the tinny chiptune of my cellphone’s 8-bit ringtone, I groaned loudly. I sat up, pushing myself off the mattress, and fumbled for the phone. The screen’s glare stung my eyes as I squinted at the caller ID.

It was Ryan. This was really not looking promising.

"Hello?" I said into the speaker, my voice harsh, cracked and husky from sleep.

"Hey bud, sorry to bother you, but it's important."

Tension crackled in Ryan Thompson’s voice, more than the usual kind of stress. Something had him really on edge.

“You good?” I asked, concern shaking off the last fog of sleep.

“Oh, I’m fine,” he said, but the words sounded hollow. “There’s been... well, something out here on the trail.”

"How delightfully unspecific." I winced. I wasn't trying to be an asshole to one of my few friends, especially my oldest friend, but in my defense, he did wake me up for this. Based on the lack of a specific name for the trail my mind defaulted to the most well-known of the region, the seaboard spanning Appalachian Trail, that ran from Georgia to Maine, and happened to go through the town in which both Ryan and I grew up. He was now an officer for the Deep Springs Police Department, a job that I was offered before I blew town right out of high school, joining the Navy and getting out of that isolated prison valley.

"Well, if I could be more specific, I would be." his voice again dipped into that well of tension, he was... disturbed by whatever was going on? I'd heard it used before when Ryan had dealt with a particularly nasty domestic abuse case that had almost ended his life back 10 years ago. "It's... a real mess up here Judah. I've got something like thirteen people dead, from some military-ish contractor types with some serious hardware to some academic types if I'm reading this situation right." Oh no. Researchers. Don't say- "We haven't found your dad at all, but we know he was with them. His gear is at the encampment, or what's left of it, but no Isaac."

Ryan’s Ask; Judah Commits to Return

November 19, 2025 Act 1 Chapter 1 Scene 1 draft 1198 words

Please leave comments on the scene using the form at the end of this section.

I groaned again. My father has been doing these little excursions up and down the Appalachian Trail as a side thing, which he doesn't need, for a while now, showing researchers and tourists the sights, smells and as he called it, the potential habitats of the deep Smoky Mountains. He'd usually walk them around the trail for a week, maybe wander deeper into the woods, scare them with some convoluted stories and bear scat as "evidence", and then bring them back to town, chattering about seeing a cryptid, or how stimulating the ideas were of the great Dr. Sands, the researcher that had one foot in the alternative narratives of the kooks and one foot in genuine research.

"So-" I started, but Ryan cut me off, his voice quick and staccato, another sign of the stress that this scene was putting him under. "No, Temperance wasn't up here with 'em. Tyler got strep a few weeks ago and she stayed behind to tend to him. So, it was just your dad and the researchers, and whoever these other guys were." I blinked, I hadn't been about to ask about Temperance, my sister-in-law and my father's "research assistant". She hadn't been mentioned, so I'd assumed she was fine. I cleared my throat and restarted my sentence. "So, these 'other guys', the military contractor-ish people, what makes you put them in that camp?" This was moving into talking cop shop, which I could do having been a Master-at-Arms, and talking shop required caffeine. So I stood up and walked to the kitchen island, loading a small plastic container into the coffee maker and setting my favorite mug on the base, it's flared, enameled mouth open like a hungry maw for the brewed caffeinated bean water. "Well, the first clue was the tactical gear," Ryan said, the question had put a problem in front of him, and he'd returned to a calmer pace and tone of voice. He thrived in puzzles and problem solving, like I do. "The second was the quality and sheer amount of ordinance they brought with them. I've got sub machine guns, carbines, pistols everywhere, by one shell count there's an estimate going around of maybe 1000 or better rounds being fired before whatever happened here was said and done? Not that it seems to have helped much." The coffee began to pour into the cup, as I sat the phone down on the counter and leaned on my palms against it. "And you said thirteen dead? how many researchers were with this group?" Ryan grunted and I could hear paper flipping, indicating that he was checking his notebook. "Well, going mostly based on who's wearing some form of tactical armor or gun, it looks like four researchers and your dad, unless I'm really missing something, leaving the nine others up here with the armed detail. I'm just going with that explanation right now, because I doubt the researchers could have put up enough of a fight to take out 9 of these guys, so it doesn't seem likely that they were opposed. Also it tracks with the amount of camping gear we found."

Jesus dad, what have you gotten yourself into? Why did it need that much security? I lifted the mug to my lips and drank deeply of the scalding bitter liquid, allowing it to burn and clarify my thoughts as I felt the heat pour down my throat into my chest. "So what do you need from me Ryan? This seems like it's already a little more than footballing a hunch session." It was Ryan's turn to clear his throat, he hated being called Johnny-on-the-spot. I picked up the phone and moved across my spartan apartment, opening the door to the patio and sitting down, indulging the salt scented spray on the brisk night breeze, and allowing the light slapping of the waves of the Elizabeth River to add to my thinking ambient noise. "The Chief isn't doing great, not that he'll admit it," That seemed the truest of truths. David Beech had been the chief of police in Deep Springs since both Ryan and I were in diapers. He was probably pushing what, 80? and was still on the job from what Ryan tells me. "And you can imagine how the Forestry Service feels about having this mess on the trail, and the Sheriff is also interested in it going away," Ryan had mentioned that the sheriff of Stratton County had been eyeing a run at their state senate seat that was coming up. It'd be a substantial boost to his political portfolio and having this case sitting out might not look great for such a 'law and order' strategist.  "I could use your help Judah. Plus, I mean, you've got Ol' Abe-" my grandfather, who wasn't doing well either according to my father's last email. "And you know how Levi Sands 1 can get, this sort of stressful situation isn't his best forte, and neither is Temperance, and this has both." And my brother and sister-in-law. Levi's response to stress, the last I heard, was to go on drug benders, and Temperance, well, "Temper" Sands the last decade or so qualified as a significant source of stress for her husband. After a beat I almost choked on my coffee. I saw where this was going. He wanted me to come down there. "Ryan-" I said warningly. "Judah." He said, adding an extra layer of dulcet to his tone and trying to up the charm. "Ryan." I said flatly. I wasn't interested in picking up that paterfamilias torch. Ryan's voice dropped nearly a full octave and picked up a dangerous edge to it. "Isaac Judah Sands Jr if you don't get your ass down here for what I can only describe as the crisis of family crisis's, I will for one disown you, and for another publish some very unflattering middle school opinions of yours that I have in a notebook still from Mrs. Johnson's 8th grade class." That son of a- "Fine. But I want that notebook." I said and hung up the phone, looking sullenly out across the river at the twinkling lights of the city. This was not how I wanted to start my retirement. "Aafo." I complained about the breeze. That small piece of the Gulf of Aden  had stayed with me, as much as the bullet lodged deep in my shoulder that caused the surrounding muscle to ache when the pressure started to change as a herald for easterly passing storms. I looked to my phone and began to make the needed purchases to make my way towards home. A plane ticket, taking me to Atlanta and then from there to Asheville, where after a few texts, it was established that I'd meet Ryan and we'd ride back to Deep Springs together and he'd catch me more up on the case where they'd leave off today. I stood and rubbed a coarse palm against my face. I guess I was up for the day, and began to get ready, loading up a duffel with clothes, my laptop, and a hard case packed Sig P320, ready for the TSA.
