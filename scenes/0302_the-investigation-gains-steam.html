The Investigation Gains Steam

November 19, 2025 Act 1 Chapter 3 Scene 2 draft 2766 words

Please leave comments on the scene using the form at the end of the page.

It took more than a few hours of going through my father's effects before I started developing the rhythm. The first and hardest part, as tended to be the case with my father, was to organize the information at hand so I knew what I was looking at, for, and with. To amuse myself, I came across a trio of statues tucked among the dozens of little baubles tucked among the books on the dusty and rough shelves and on the flat surfaces of low tables. A sextant he'd purchased on a trip in Portugal, an idol from a marketplace in the Yucatan, a replica organ container from Cairo, he was never satisfied with a trip unless he had something tangible to remember it by. That thought led to a mild burn starting behind my eyes, I wasn't one to be that in touch with my feelings, but at a guess this was the overwhelming sadness that would cause people in a similar situation to have the moments of despondence that I couldn't afford. I dragged in a deep and suspiciously ragged breath, forcing the feeling into the box so I could work. I guess in the present circumstance he'd brought something tangible for us to experience from him. Since for the moment this was my office until we found him alive and well. I picked one, the looked to be a period farmer with a rifle slung high on his shoulder at the ready, his eyes shielded beneath a broad brimmed hat, and decided he looked like what I would associate as someone who was "Looking For". A lost calf, a son, prey on a hunt. I smiled to myself at the comparison. Not a farmer. A hunter. I placed him on the far left of the hutch of the desk, right above a paper sized cubby, shuffling a stack of papers into the void under the figurine. These were documents that referenced things like "Gwynporth" which seemed to be some kind of location that Corven and my Father thought was near to the town, and "Drywds" which just operating by cognate logic suggested that my father and the Cryptozoological mogul were looking for Druids in these mountains which struck me as more preposterous than even dad would take out for a lark. Another, an older looking figurine of a slighter man, knelt with a spyglass seemed very appropriate for a "Looking For" and I set him to the center of the desk. Something about the figurine peering through the spyglass seemed appropriate for what I was going for. Under "For" I had my legal pad with a series of questions written down on it "Where was dad going?", "Did they find the site they were looking for?" etc. The final one I set on the hutch above the laptop, A matronly figure, dressed in a long dress and apron, much akin to what the old folks would call a Granny, with a basket of herbs crooked in her arm. I deemed her as "Looking With" for the trio, and anything I brought to the desk to try to solve this mystery was under her domain. Pleased with my organizational in-joke, I pulled papers from the “For” cubby. They were a shabby mix—folded corners, bent edges, and brittle pages bearing the marks of long years spent scattered on my father’s desk. Some were older reference materials, photocopied from books stored in climate-controlled archives my father had once frequented. Others were newer, cross-referenced against the overstuffed shelves that groaned under the weight of his personal library. I sat back in my father’s—no, my—creaking bank chair, its metallic springs straining with each shift. The seat groaned in harmony with the faint squeal of the ceiling fan overhead, stirring the dead, stale air. The study felt like something between a library and a warehouse—part intellectual haven, part storeroom for treasures that might have been plucked from Raiders of the Lost Ark. The expansive executive desk, worn and imposing, anchored the room. My father had bought it when he retired from academia, a relic of his transition from professor to full-time adventurer. I opened its drawers, the wood sticking slightly as though reluctant to give up its secrets. The muscles in my face twitched a little as I saw the paper on the top of the stack in one of the drawers. It was clean and white, like it was a fresh copy, but I could see the header and footer lines that told me it was a faxed document to my father. I glanced at the printer that my nephew had told me they had recently set up in the office, and sure enough from the angle to it I was, I could see the phone jack plugged into the side, allowing for faxes to come through. The image on the document was, alarming. A scrawled hand written note that said "THE SEEKER IS WATCHED. THE WATCHER WAITS." I read the page twice, and a third time, the hair on my neck raising with each successive read. Was dad being threatened? Who had sent this? The letters themselves were scrawled and jagged, like each stroke was a threat made into graphic. I looked at the backside of the paper. Blank. Except for a small header note on that side that had the number that the image had been faxed from, and a date, 10/16/25. Just the day before my father left on his expedition. That couldn't be a coincidence. The hum of the ceiling fan droned on over my thoughts, along with it's periodic squeak of protest from moving the still all too still air in the room. The clatter from the window was an explosion of sound. Heavy, Sharp, Metallic. As if someone had thrown something through the glass of the window. I jerked upright out of the bankers chair, sending it rolling away from the back of my legs as I drew a bead on the source of the noise, my service weapon in my hand without the line of thoughts to instruct my hands and arms to do so. My ears thumping with my own heart beat and adrenaline laced blood. Heart beating against my ribs, I moved closer to the window to find the source of the noise. As I got close enough to the glass, I saw my own reflection, eyes flared open, the whites standing out starkly as I stared. Then another flash of movement drew my attention. A blur of blue, grey, black and white plumage came to a stop on the window sill and stared back at me through the glass. A blue jay. Breathing heavily, I holstered the gun. With the amount of weirdness going on, and a targeted threat to my father, it seemed like I couldn't be too safe I guess and my defensive reflexes went off. I stared at the Jay and it stared back at me, seemingly unphased by me being just on the other side of the glass from it. It cocked it's head, almost inviting me to speak, and before I could think about anything "...Velandor?" escaped from my lips. I heard the 'keue' of the Blue Jay respond approvingly through the window, as it flitted away, back into the darkness. I shook my head. "What is a Velandor?" I pondered to the empty room, my hands moving to the bracelet on my wrist, rubbing the Amethyst embedded in the leather with a practiced preoccupation. The stone had come from my mother's necklace after she'd died, not that I was particularly superstitious, but it felt nice to have a physical connection to her still. I found it soothing. I glanced back at the fax, now sitting on the floor from the interruption. Why did the Watcher and this mysterious unbidden word seem connected. I couldn't wrap words around it as to why, but something instinctual screamed at me from deep inside that these two things were connected. I rubbed at the stone some more in thought, feeling the cool surface under my fingertips. I shook my head and cleared my mind from the distraction. I couldn't afford it. I crouched and picked up the fax, careful to keep it in the same condition that I'd found it. I stood at the desk and jotted down the fax number from the header of the sheet onto my legal pad, and then located the chair I'd been using and drew it back under me as i booted on the laptop. Let's see if I could figure out who had sent this fax.

It took some doing, but I eventually found a listing on a message board that directed me to a source for the number on the fax header. It had been for a "S. Swan Thirdhand Thrift and Goode Co." that was listed in Walnut. I'd just jotted down the number when I heard the soft, deliberate padding footsteps of Temperance approaching the door to the study. The door creaked open, the hinges sounding like they had been original to a much older house than this, the metal squealing as it grinded against itself to allow entry. Temperance for her part was put together for the day, her curly copper hair tied up in a long ponytail behind her over her hoodie, her left hand holding a plate with a sandwich aloft. "I might be mad, but I'm not going to let someone starve under my roof." She said curtly as she crossed the room to the edge of the desk to set down the plate. "What are you working-" She stopped cold as she saw the fax, and the scrawled words upon it. "What the hell? What is this supposed to be?" I sighed, drumming my fingers on the legal pad next to the laptop. Damn it all I was tired. "Either a warning, or a threat, sent two days before Dad headed out." I looked up and locked eyes with Temperance. "Best I can figure out the seeker is meant to be Dad, and I'm not sure who the Watcher is referring to. I hate to be like this, but has anyone been, I don't know, threatening dad more broadly than an anonymous fax?" Temperance pursed her lips, and knitted her brows in annoyance. "Two Days? Was it still on the printer or-?" "No, it was tucked in one of the drawers. So I assume Dad did what Dad does, and saw it and ignored it." I raised my hands in exasperation and leaned back in the chair. "Probably assumed it was a crank and nothing would come of it." "Of course he did. I mean, if you wanted a narrow list of people that would threaten him, you are out of luck." Her annoyance made the jab clear in her voice, even if the appended "Because you weren't here to know about it" wasn't stated. "Probably not the Puritans over in Town of Joe, they'd not have bothered with the fax and just done something like try to ruin Isaac's reputation, then it's maybe another online Cryptid Hunter that he debunked a video of them saying something about their favorite cryptid. If not them, it's someone around here who he's pissed off by being either too involved in local goings on or not involved enough depending on the person. He wasn't exactly someone that minded the Ps and Qs of the status quo around here Judah." I nodded. That had always been true of dad, both when I was a kid and after I got out into the world. He'd never been one to let anyone else dictate how he felt, or talked about a subject. "Well, one, Puritans? I thought they were in Massachusetts, and two, So not narrowing it down to less than half the county. Lets add a variable. What about the--" I stole a glance to the legal pad to make sure I got the name right, "--Swan Thirdhand Thrift and Goode Co. With an E?" Temperance blinked. "Swan? The old guy in Walnut? I don't think he ever seemed to have a beef with him. Isaac's been going over there a lot lately though. Right after this expedition contract landed. Maybe he though Mister Stefan knew something?" "I mean maybe. I don't actually know a lot about him, Grandpa visited him with me a couple of times, but I usually was distracted by all the stuff in there and didn't really pay attention to anything they were saying to each other." I frowned. I shouldn't blame child me for not paying attention, what child would pay attention to what two old men were gossiping about. Especially in the presence of nigh ancient bric-a-brac lining every wall and shelf, begging to be taken down and played with. "Well, I ask because that's what I found this number tied to. According to the 'Dusty Vintage Relics' message board, someone has said it's the fax number to the place." Temperance smiled a bemused smile. "I mean, Mister Stefan is pretty old, I can't think of anyone that would still have a working fax number." She shot a look to the pristine printer, standing out as the new marvel of technology among my father's effects in the library. "Outside of Isaac apparently." I smirked at the jab towards my father. "So, operating on them being friends, or at least business friendly, I'd think this would be more a warning than a threat. I think I need to go up there and see Swan for myself." Temperance quirked an ill-tempered eyebrow. "Judah, are you sure that's a good idea? Not like you've got a healthy reputation around here at all, and there's only so far that Isaac's coattails can take you." She shifted her weight and cocked her head her expression softening from a steely sarcasm to something more akin to comradery and concern, while the shifting of her head sent the waves of copper from her ponytail shimmering over her shoulder. "Not that it's an unhealthy one, just it doesn't really exist at all around here." She looked down at the sandwich that remained untouched on the plate. "Also eat. You aren't going anywhere until you take care of yourself some and stop looking like a half starved possum that wandered in from the woods." I begrudgingly lifted the sandwich and took a bite. The taste of baloney and mustard filled my mouth, which caused a deep gurgling reaction in my stomach. How long had it been since I'd eaten? I chewed thoughtfully and swallowed. "Yeah, I guess I don't really have much of one, but maybe that will be more helpful than not. I also don't have the same rankles that Dad did with people. Or, more to the point, I'm not important enough to anyone to truly annoy them yet." I paused, taking another thoughtful bite of the sandwich. She wasn't wrong. I was basically a ghost in this town, more a name and memory rather than a person. Maybe I could work that to my advantage, or maybe it just meant no one would care what this sudden new Sands would uncover. I scarfed down more of the sandwich, and asked "So, this Stefan Swan, do you know him any?" Temperance slid off the desk and into one of the chairs. "Not very well. Enough to know that he's legit on his history. Old enough to have probably lived through most of it, at least according to him." she offered a rare wry smile to me. "He's not without his quirks, like Isaac. Swears by a lot of odd old stories. Knows more than he lets on. You might be surprised." I looked up as I finished my sandwich, against locking my eyes against Temperance's viridian ones. "Honestly, I'm not sure anyone up here surprises me at this point." I started to collect the papers I had questions on from around the desktop, neatly stacking them on the legal pad a couple of pages from the top. "But, if Stefan Swan has potential answers, I guess I need to go see him." Temperance stood up as I did and started heading for the door to the study. "Just don't try to scare him off. He might scare you back." she said. The lilt of a joke in the tone of her voice as she disappeared down the hallway. I picked up the keys to the Bronco and started striding towards the door myself. The watcher waits. Well if someone's watching. It's time they saw this seeker seek.
