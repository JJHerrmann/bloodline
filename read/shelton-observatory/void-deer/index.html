CASE FILE SO-02-VD-12

THE VOID DEER

Primary subject: Andrew Mullins Incident type: Fatal Encounter / Anomalous Predation Provisional class: Predatory Manifestation Hazard grade: IV - Lethal Influence Confirmed Local designation: "The Void Deer" Location: Sandy Mush, North Carolina Initial encounter: December 12, 2002 Associated fatality: Clifford Andrews Interview recorded: December 14, 2010`

Interview subject: Andrew Mullins Interview conducted by: Judith Bell, Associate Researcher

Case status: Unresolved Entity status: Confirmed Access: Internal Research

SO-02-VD-12-A - MULLINS INTERVIEW

[INTERVIEW BEGINS]

BELL: Judith Bell, Associate Researcher at the Shelton Observatory, interviewing an Andrew Mullins in regard to alleged supernatural phenomena that were encountered on December 12th, 2002. Mr. Mullins appears before me, looking around his mid-40s. His license, provided to confirm his identification, confirms his birthdate as September 12th, 1974. Mister Mullins?

MULLINS: Yes ma'am.

BELL: You are 36 years old?

MULLINS: By birth, yes.

BELL: And yet-

MULLINS: And yet, here I am, bald pate and the beginning stages of osteoarthritis. Mother always said I'd get it too. I guess she was right.

BELL: And how old would you qualify yourself as physically, Mr. Mullins?

MULLINS: Easily... 45? 50?

BELL: And you say the reason that you believe this has to do with an encounter you had eight years ago, back in 2002?

MULLINS: It absolutely did, ma'am. I was there.

BELL: Why don't you tell me about it.

MULLINS: Well. Back in 02, my buddy Cliff and I were pretty avid deer hunters. We'd get to the weekend during the season, leave on Friday, come back with a couple head by Sunday evening to clean em up. So one Friday, early December, me and Cliff went out towards Sandy Mush. Good deer there then, you know.

So we got to this little place that Cliff knowed, and set up. I was settled in, had a thermos and all, waiting on the first bucks of the day. Hadn't even really started yet coming daylight. Now, from where I was, I could see Cliff's stand. We were across from each other, but staggered like to not be in the line of fire of the other.

So I'm sitting in my blind, enjoying the quiet, when I see movement and realize Cliff's climbed out of his blind back to the ground. Nothing major, probably had to pee and wanted to move far enough out so he didn't accidentally mark his blind. But then he starts screaming.

BELL: Screaming? Like he was in danger?

MULLINS: Unless he was just trying to scare off the deer, that's what I'd assumed. So I released the chambered round I'd had, cleared the gun, and looked out with the rifle. Had a new scope on it that'd said it was good for night and all, and it seemed like an ideal time, since I'd be able to catch him and see what was going on. And I did. He was standing, about 30 ft into the tree line, pants around his ankles at that point, staring up at the biggest damned buck I'd ever seen. I'd have called it a moose, but we definitely don't have those here. And that's not even the part that got me.

BELL: Something else then?

MULLINS: Well, it was a big damn buck, but it was also white as a sheet. And when I looked through the scope, it looked dead at me. Like it knew I was watching. Then it opened its mouth.

BELL: Was there something wrong with-

MULLINS: Damn right there was. Deer's got little flat teeth. Made for plants. This thing. It had knives for teeth. Well, at that point Cliff started hollerin again, cause of course you would, seein that up close. I about hollered and I was across a field from it. That's when I started feeling... tired. Worn out.

But I couldn't let Cliff, who'd probably left his gun up in the stand blind, to just be next to that thing, so I chambered a round and fired at the thing. I know it ain't right with Cliff being right there, but I figured between me and that, he was probably in more danger of it than me.

Now. I hit it. I seen it hit. Not a bragging hunter thing, but hand to God, I saw that thing take that bullet to the neck.

Nothing. Happened.

Then it smiled. Now deer don't smile. Dogs smile. Cats smile. But Deer don't do that.

And I couldn't look away.

I couldn't take my eye off the scope, and I couldn't get my fingers to work getting a new round in the chamber to try again. And I tried. Like muscles burning trying to get enough hold on a round to load it, and I just kept dropping them.

That's when-

BELL: That's when?

MULLINS: Yeah. Cliff started really screaming then. And his legs finally started workin, despite his britches being down. He started to jet towards the field. Now the thing... the Deer, didn't start running after him.

BELL: So it just let him go?

MULLINS: I wish. It started just walking behind him. Like it knewed he wouldn't get nowhere. And it was right, cause about thens when Cliff tripped into a bush, catching his pants and all. He went down behind a thicket and I couldn't see him no more, but I could see it. I could see it walk up to the thicket and lower its head.

Cliff screamed once. And I'll never unhear that. I'll be dead and still hearing it.

And that crowned head came up, painted red.

Well, now I've done pissed myself, cause I just watched all that, and I practically throw the gun out of the blind just to get myself to stop watching about the time it lowered its head again.

Now I'm shaking like one of them lil dogs up in my stand. Do I go down? Do I get the gun? Maybe run over there and try to help Cliff?

BELL: But you didn't, I take it.

MULLINS: Naw. I sat in the blind and bawled. I was so fuckin scared. I'm curled around the platform of this thing trying to be as quiet as I can lest I get its attention next.

Then I hear scraping under the stand. And-

BELL: Actually. Let's take a break there. Before I can't sleep tonight.

MULLINS: Should have been there. I avoid sleeping when I can.

BELL: So when did you notice this... accelerated aging?

MULLINS: Not til after, for sure. I don't know if being that scared did it, or if that... thing did. But sitting up in that stand hearing it trying to saw at the ropes and shit holding the stand up in the air... well.

BELL: Well, um. So you noticed the accelerated aging after the encounter?

MULLINS: Yeah. After It moved off, mid-day or so, I got out of the blind and scrambled down to the gun, and when I went to grab it, that spot. The one right here on my hand. was when I noticed. Cause it hadn't been there when I'd gone up the tree. I called the sheriff's office, and Deputy Reynolds came around, and called the wildlife guys. None of them would believe me when I told em a deer'd done what'd been done to Cliff. Kept saying I must have mistook a bear or something.

That weren't no bear. Weren't no cat. Weren't no deer either.

[END OF INTERVIEW]

APPENDICES

SO-02-VD-12-B - N.C. Wildlife Resources Commission Report

Reviewing the attached documents, the NC Wildlife Commission Officer, Daniel Wendell, states that he concurred with the deputy that the prevailing identification of a massive albino deer was in error, and that the wounds on the discovered cadaver were more consistent with teeth and claws from an ursid or feline attacker. SO-02-VD-12-C - Stratton County Sheriff's Office Action Report

The Sheriff's Deputy, a Jefferson Reynolds, formerly of the Stratton County Sheriff's office, and unavailable for further comment, indicated that when he'd found Mister Mullins, he reeked of urine, and was in a crazed state. To the point where Deputy Reynolds had considered holding him pending a drug and field sobriety test. Reynolds indicated that he removed the gun from Mister Mullin, and approached the thicket that Mister Mullin would not go near, but indicated that his friend was. The expositive summary of the report indicates that Reynolds, based on Mullins' behavior and temperment upon contact, was under the assumption that Mullins himself my have killed Mister Andrews in a drug haze, and only thought he'd seen a deer. However the Sheriff's description of the injuries sustained to Mister Andrews are in-line with the Wildlife Resources Commission's interpretation, of something akin to a feline or ursid was the preliminary COD for Mister Andrews.

The summary also notes that some of Reynolds' hesitation in regards to the account of Mister Mullin stemps from past encounters with authorities, inlcuding for several juvienile and post-maturity drug charges.

Original File is under SO-02-VD-12-C.1

SO-02-VD-12-D Clifford James Andrews, N.C. OCME autopsy report

Researcher's Note: I tried to read this one as little as possible outside of the specific details I wanted for this report. Mister Mullins' story still sits with me enough that I did not wish to antagonize the issue further.

The N.C. OCME autopsy report lists animal attack, and rapid exsanguination as the causes of death for Mister Andrews. The cornor also reports that blood toxcity did contain evidence of alcohol consumption, but with the loss of such an amount of blood the coroner found it insufficient to say if Mister Andrews was beyond the legal limit for hunting.
